## 1. Scope & the four reference houses

Four luxury beauty houses define the visual grammar this studio studies and adapts — never
copies — for **زيت المشاط الأحمر** (*zayt al-mishāṭ al-aḥmar*, "Red Mechat Oil", Bible §3). Each
has a recognizable "code": a repeatable set of shot choices, pacing, and material treatment that
reads as premium at a glance. The table names what each house is known for, and what the
flagship **UNBROKEN THREAD** (Bible §6) borrows versus what it deliberately leaves behind.

| House | Signature craft codes (industry-recognized) | What UNBROKEN THREAD borrows | What it leaves behind |
|-------|-----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|------------------------|
| **L'Oréal** (Elseve/Elvive, "Worth It" campaigns) | Hero-glam turn-to-camera, wraparound beauty light, hair whip/toss, testimonial-confidence address, before/after split-screen | The **hero-glam turn** (§6.1 TRANSFORMATION, 5.2–6.6 s) and the **wraparound beauty light** on the keeper | **Split-screen before/after** and **hair whip/toss** — both read as mass-market/dropshipping tells; the flagship shows transformation through **one continuous camera thread**, not a cut or a whip (10-luxury-commercial-director §3.7 cheap-ad tells) |
| **Dior** (J'adore, Sauvage flacon films) | Jewel product macro, gold/liquid pour in slow motion, product **orbit** reveal on a plinth, "the walk" (a model walking an opulent set toward camera before the reveal) | **Jewel product macro** (bottle as a ruby flacon, §4), the **liquid pour** (§6.1 DISCOVERY 1.6–3.0 s), the **orbit** (§6.1 OFFER+CTA, 7.0–8.0 s, bottle orbits then the 3-pack rises) | **"The walk"** — the flagship has no model traversing a set; the camera itself is the unbroken traveler, so the walk is replaced by **one continuous move** (Bible §6, keeper principle) |
| **Chanel** (No. 5, Coco Mademoiselle) | Editorial restraint, negative space, a single accent color, one hard specular, minimal typography, "shown not shouted" | **Restraint and negative space** at every beat (10-luxury-commercial-director §3.3), **one hero per frame**, the calm, unbadged offer card | Chanel's monochrome/near-B&W palette — the flagship stays in its warm garnet/cream/gold register (§3); Chanel's total silence — the flagship keeps a single warm musical motif (§6.5) because sound-on Gulf social video needs an emotional anchor |
| **La Mer** (Crème de la Mer, "Miracle Broth" films) | Marine/mineral macro texture, dewy translucent product macro, glass-jar macro, spa-calm unhurried pacing, near-ASMR sound design (spoon, jar, water) | **Dewy translucent macro** (the garnet oil read as jewel-liquid, §4), **spa-calm unhurried pacing** (24 fps, one move, no jitter — Bible §6.1), the **intimate, near-ASMR sound layer** (§6.5: breath, glassy tone, ripple, whoosh, clink, chime) | La Mer's cool marine palette (teal/aqua) — the flagship is warm garnet/gold, never cool or oceanic (§3) |

**Working rule:** the flagship never *quotes* a house's signature verbatim (no split-screen, no
hair-whip, no walk, no cool-marine grade). It extracts the **underlying craft principle** —
macro intimacy, jewel product handling, restraint, unhurried pacing — and renders it inside the
Bible's own locks (§3/§4/§5). This is adaptation, not pastiche.

## 2. Shot-type grammar

The vocabulary of shots luxury beauty commercials are built from, what each is *for*, which
house exemplifies it, its typical screen time in a luxury :15–:30 spot, and where it appears in
the flagship's 8 s primary cut (Bible §6.1).

| Shot type | Definition / technique | House exemplar | Typical duration (luxury spot) | UNBROKEN THREAD usage |
|-----------|--------------------------|-----------------|----------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Extreme macro / insert** | Lens fills frame with one small detail (a droplet, a strand, a pore); shallow DoF isolates it from everything else | La Mer (cream texture), Dior (liquid pour) | 0.5–2.0 s per insert | HOOK comb/strand macro (0.0–1.2 s); pour-into-oil macro (3.0–3.4 s); RITUAL fingertip macro (3.4–4.8 s) |
| **Hero product shot** | The product centered, held still or on a slow move, label legible, one clean specular | Dior (flacon on plinth), Chanel (bottle in negative space) | 2–4 s | DISCOVERY reveal (1.6–3.0 s); OFFER+CTA hero pack (7.0–8.0 s) |
| **Beauty pass** | A slow, continuous camera glide across a face, hand, or hair section that lets light travel across the surface, revealing texture and luminosity as it moves | L'Oréal ("worth it" glow reveal), La Mer (skin macro) | 1.5–3 s | TRANSFORMATION turn (5.2–6.6 s) — the shine ribbon *is* a beauty pass across the hair as she turns |
| **Turn-to-camera** | Subject rotates from profile/away toward lens, timed to land the reveal on a beat of music or VO | L'Oréal (the "reveal" turn), Chanel (the glance-back) | 1–2 s | TRANSFORMATION (5.2–6.6 s): natural over-the-shoulder turn to camera |
| **Product orbit** | Camera arcs around a static product on a plinth/podium at a slow, even angular velocity | Dior (flacon orbit), most fragrance/skincare hero shots | 1.5–3 s | OFFER+CTA (7.0–8.0 s): camera orbits the single bottle; mid-orbit the 3-pack assembles |
| **Pour / liquid shot** | A controlled pour or drop filmed macro, often in slow motion, to show viscosity and color as a proof of formula richness | Dior (golden pour), La Mer (broth spoon) | 1–2 s | DISCOVERY pour (part of 1.6–3.0 s) and the dive-into-pour transition (3.0–3.4 s) |
| **Rack focus** | Focus racks from a foreground detail to a background subject (or reverse) within one continuous shot, substituting for a cut | Chanel, Dior (product-to-face or face-to-product reveals) | 0.5–1.5 s | Implicit inside the continuous camera thread as it moves from oil surface up to the bottle (1.2–1.6 s transition) |
| **Over-the-shoulder reveal** | Camera positioned behind/beside the subject as she turns, so the reveal is felt as discovered, not presented | L'Oréal, Chanel | 1.5–2.5 s | TRANSFORMATION (5.2–6.6 s) — explicitly an over-the-shoulder turn |
| **Match / liquid-morph transition** | One element's shape, color, or motion is matched to the next shot's opening frame so the cut reads as continuous, not edited | Dior/Chanel high-end transitions; increasingly a "one-take" luxury signature | 0.2–0.6 s per match | Every one of the flagship's four transitions (1.2–1.6 s drop→oil, 3.0–3.4 s oil→scalp, 4.8–5.2 s hair-sweep, 6.6–7.0 s light-bloom) — the studio's core device (Bible §6, "the oil drop and the hair strand are the transition engines") |
| **Light bloom / flare insert** | A soft, motivated light flare used as a bridge between two beats, always traced to a light source already in frame | Dior (glint on glass), high-end fragrance transitions | 0.3–0.5 s | 6.6–7.0 s: the golden highlight travelling her hair blooms into a soft flare, bridging to the product |
| **Wide establishing shot** | A full-set wide that orients the viewer in space | Rare in luxury beauty; more common in prestige fragrance films with "the walk" | 1–3 s when used | **Not used** — the flagship's 8 s runtime has no spare beat for orientation; the set is always felt through macro and mid, never established wide |

## 3. Pacing & rhythm

Pacing is the fastest tell of production value. Luxury beauty commercials move **slower and with
fewer cuts** than performance-marketing creative, and every luxury house shares the same core
disciplines, which the flagship inherits inside the Bible's platform rules (§5).

**The four pacing disciplines (shared across all four houses):**

1. **Low cut count, high shot duration.** A :15 luxury spot rarely exceeds 6–9 shots; performance
   ads often carry 15–20 in the same runtime. The flagship's entire 8 s primary cut is **one
   continuous camera move with zero hard cuts** — the most extreme version of this discipline,
   achievable only because transitions are physical (match/morph), not edited (§6.1).
2. **24 fps film cadence.** All four houses shoot (or grade to) 24 fps for the filmic, "shot on
   film" feel — never the 60 fps "phone video" smoothness of UGC/performance creative. The
   flagship locks to the 24–30 fps band with a 24 fps priority (Bible §5).
3. **Slow motion is rationed, not blanket.** Luxury spots reserve slow-mo for **one** emotionally
   loaded beat (a pour, a fabric fall, a hair flip) — not the whole film. The flagship reserves
   its slow-mo for the falling strand only (HOOK, 0.0–1.2 s); every other beat runs at natural
   speed inside the single continuous move.
4. **The hold.** Luxury pacing lets an emotional beat *land* — a beat of stillness after the
   reveal before the next cut/move, letting a real blink or breath read as human, not rushed. The
   flagship's TRANSFORMATION (5.2–6.6 s, 1.4 s) is sized specifically to hold the turn, the
   micro-smile, one real blink, and one real breath (Bible §6.1) — 1.4 s is roughly the minimum
   luxury beauty commercials allow for a reveal to feel earned rather than flashed.

**Typical shot-duration bands by beat type (from luxury spot analysis), and the flagship's own
timing against them:**

| Beat type | Typical luxury-spot duration | Flagship duration | Note |
|-----------|-------------------------------|----------------------|------|
| Macro/insert (pain, texture, detail) | 0.8–2.0 s | 1.2 s (HOOK) | Inside band; sized to also serve as Meta/TikTok/Snap's hook window (§5) |
| Product reveal | 1.5–3.0 s | 1.4 s (DISCOVERY, 1.6–3.0 s) | At the tight end of the band — an 8 s runtime cannot afford a longer dwell |
| Sensory/ritual macro | 1.0–2.5 s | 1.4 s (RITUAL, 3.4–4.8 s) | Inside band |
| Transformation/hero-glam | 1.0–2.0 s | 1.4 s (TRANSFORMATION, 5.2–6.6 s) | Inside band; the studio's single glow peak (10-luxury-commercial-director R5) |
| Offer/CTA | 1.0–1.5 s | 1.0 s (OFFER+CTA, 7.0–8.0 s) | At the tight end — calm, not rushed, per §5's "brand + offer legible in the first and last second" |

**Silence as pacing.** All four houses treat empty sonic space as a luxury signal — music that
breathes rather than a wall-to-wall bed. The flagship's ~70–85 BPM oud/qanun motif carries **one
resolving swell exactly on the transformation** (Bible §6.5) and rests elsewhere, mirroring La
Mer's and Chanel's restrained scoring more than L'Oréal's fuller "uplift" beds.

## 4. Macro-beauty language (the vocabulary)

The words an agency shot list or director's brief uses to specify a macro-beauty shot so a
cinematographer, colorist, and (in this studio) a prompt compiler render the same intent. Grouped
by subject; each entry is the term plus what it visually specifies. These are the words
09-beauty-commercial-director and 26-kie-prompt-builder should reach for when writing prompt
language, not invented synonyms.

**Liquid / oil vocabulary:**

| Term | Specifies |
|------|-----------|
| **Ribbon (pour)** | A continuous, unbroken stream of liquid, not droplets — reads as controlled, generous, premium |
| **Translucent / jewel-clear** | Light passes through the liquid; it is not opaque paint — the core descriptor for the garnet oil (§4) |
| **Viscosity read** | The liquid's fall/pour speed communicates richness — a too-fast, water-thin pour reads cheap; a controlled, slightly weighted fall reads rich |
| **Suspended highlight** | A point of light caught and held inside the liquid body (not just on its surface) — signals depth, not a flat color card |
| **Bead / droplet** | A single, discrete sphere of liquid (as opposed to a ribbon) — used for the falling oil drop that meets the falling strand (§6.1 transition) |
| **Surface tension** | The liquid's skin holds its shape at the pour's edge before breaking — a realism cue against a "CGI liquid metal" look (10-luxury-commercial-director §6.6) |

**Skin vocabulary:**

| Term | Specifies |
|------|-----------|
| **Lit-from-within / subsurface glow** | Light appears to originate under the skin, not bounce off it — the L'Oréal "worth it" glow, rendered with pores intact |
| **Beauty light / wraparound light** | A soft, broad key that wraps the face without hard shadow — the classic beauty-commercial lighting shape |
| **Soft specular** | A gentle, not blown, highlight on the high points of the face (cheekbone, brow, cupid's bow) |
| **Micro-texture** | Visible pores, fine vellus (peach-fuzz) hair — the opposite of "airbrushed," and the studio's core anti-AI-tell defense (§6.6 `plastic/waxy skin`) |
| **Practical contact** | Real fingertip-to-skin contact with visible pressure/give — as opposed to a hand miming over the surface |

**Hair vocabulary:**

| Term | Specifies |
|------|-----------|
| **Shine ribbon** | A soft, *moving* band of specular highlight that travels along the strand as the hair moves — the visual proof of a shine claim |
| **Strand definition** | Individual strands resolve distinctly, with depth from root to tip — as opposed to a "melted" hair mass |
| **Flyaway** | One or two individual loose strands outside the main mass — a deliberate realism cue against a too-perfect "helmet" look |
| **Weight / bounce** | Hair motion has mass and follows physics (settles after a turn) rather than floating or snapping rigidly |
| **Root-to-tip gradient** | A natural tonal shift from darker roots to warmer, lighter reflections along the length — reads as healthy, not uniform/synthetic |

**Product-macro vocabulary:**

| Term | Specifies |
|------|-----------|
| **Hero handling** | Slow, controlled, confident hand movement on the product — never fumbled or rushed |
| **Label-to-camera** | The product is rotated/held so the label reads clearly to the lens at the reveal's peak — non-negotiable for brand recall |
| **One clean specular** | A single, motivated highlight on the glass — multiple competing highlights read as over-lit/cheap |

**Flagship application in one line each:** the HOOK strand is macro with *strand definition* and
a *flyaway* or two; the falling oil is a *bead* that meets it; DISCOVERY is *hero handling* with
*label-to-camera*; the pour is a *ribbon* with a *suspended highlight*; RITUAL is *practical
contact* on skin and a *root-to-tip gradient* on the hair section; TRANSFORMATION is the *shine
ribbon* traveling under *lit-from-within* skin.

## 5. Product-hero conventions

How luxury beauty commercials physically treat the product as the protagonist — the conventions
that separate a "hero shot" from a generic product-on-a-table insert, mapped to the §4 product
lock.

| Convention | What it means | Reference-house source | Flagship execution (§4 lock intact) |
|------------|----------------|--------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **The product enters, it is not discovered pre-placed** | A hand, a pour, or a camera move *reveals* the product rather than opening on it sitting static — creates a small narrative beat around the object | Dior (flacon lifted into frame), Chanel | An elegant warm-skinned hand enters and lifts the Mechat bottle (§6.1 DISCOVERY, 1.6–3.0 s) |
| **One light, one specular** | The product is lit so exactly one clean highlight sits on the glass — never a flat, shadowless product-photography wash | All four houses | Warm arch light (§4 hero-environment DNA) produces one motivated key; no ring-light catchlights (10-luxury-commercial-director R3) |
| **Label-to-camera at the peak** | Somewhere in the hero beat, the label is rotated fully legible to lens, held long enough to register | All four houses (brand recall is non-negotiable) | "label to camera" is explicit in the beat sheet (§6.1 DISCOVERY) |
| **The product occupies a minority of frame** | Negative space around the hero product signals confidence — a product that must fill the frame to be noticed reads as insecure/cheap | Chanel (extreme), Dior | Bottle placed in the warm cream arch field with the garnet drape and single hibiscus — never edge-to-edge (10-luxury-commercial-director §3.3) |
| **Multiplication reads as abundance, not clutter — if staged as one beat** | Showing the full pack (not just the single hero unit) communicates value, but only when staged as a single deliberate reveal, not a shelf | Skincare "regimen" hero shots (La Mer sets), Dior gift-set films | The 3-pack rises into place *mid-orbit* as one continuous move (§6.1 OFFER+CTA) — never cut to a separate "shelf" shot |
| **The pour/application is filmed as proof, not decoration** | A pour or application shot exists to demonstrate the formula's real behavior (viscosity, coverage), not merely to look pretty | Dior (golden pour), La Mer (broth texture) | The oil pour (DISCOVERY) and the RITUAL fingertip application are both framed as sensory proof of the §3 claims (coats, silky, non-greasy), never purely decorative |
| **The cap and closure are never obscured at the reveal** | A luxury product's hardware (cap, closure, seal) is part of its identity and stays legible | All four houses | The matte white ribbed cap and the gold «طبيعي 100٪» (*ṭabīʿī miʾa bi-l-miʾa*, "100% natural") seal stay legible through DISCOVERY and OFFER+CTA (§4) — never cropped or shadowed into anonymity |

## 6. Material richness & restraint

Luxury reads through **material truth**, not gloss, and through **what is absent** as much as
what is present. This section pairs the two because in practice they are one craft: an
uncluttered frame is what lets a real material register.

**Material-richness codes (how each material is rendered "rich but real" across the four
houses, and in the flagship — full detail lives in 10-luxury-commercial-director §3.6):**

| Material | Rich-but-real treatment | Cheap/fake failure mode |
|----------|----------------------------|----------------------------|
| Glass / PET | Clean, real reflections of the actual set around it | Fake mirror-gloss, plastic-toy sheen |
| Liquid | Translucent, viscosity visible, one suspended highlight | Opaque flat color, "CGI liquid metal" |
| Hair | Individual strands, warm reflections, natural weight | Melted mass, wet-look helmet, plastic sheen |
| Skin | Subsurface glow, visible pores | Airbrushed wax, "beauty filter" doll skin |
| Stone / travertine | Matte grain, honest cast shadow | Glossy plastic pedestal, no contact shadow |
| Textile | Deep matte fabric, soft real folds | Shiny satin cliché, garish pattern |
| Botanical | Fresh, single, deliberately placed | A bouquet (clutter), wilted or plastic-looking |
| Metal (the gold seal) | Small, warm, restrained | Oversized shiny badge, loud foil |

**Restraint codes (what the four houses remove that a generic ad keeps):**

- **One hero per frame.** Exactly one subject owns the frame; everything else is a supporting
  field, not a competitor for attention.
- **Negative space is load-bearing, not empty.** The cream/void around the product or face is
  doing narrative work (calm, confidence) — it is never "dead space" to be filled with a graphic.
- **≤ one overlay per beat.** Luxury type treatment is one short line, set once, never stacked
  captions or a checklist.
- **No unmotivated light.** Every highlight, rim, or flare traces to a light source that exists in
  the world of the shot (a window, a practical lamp) — never a floating catchlight.
- **No decoration that isn't proof.** A sparkle, a swirl graphic, or a floating ingredient icon
  substitutes for a real sensory shot; luxury craft always prefers the real macro over the
  graphic stand-in.

These restraint codes are the **flagship's negative-space discipline** (10-luxury-commercial-
director §3.1–§3.3) applied one level up, as general luxury-beauty craft rather than
flagship-specific direction — this document is the *why it's an industry-wide code*; that skill
is the *how it's executed beat-by-beat*.

## 8. Glossary of agency vocabulary

Terms used in luxury beauty commercial briefs, shot lists, and this studio's own skill outputs
(09–15, 26). Alphabetical; each definition is generic craft knowledge, not a flagship-specific
invention.

- **Beauty light** — a soft, broad key light shaped to wrap a face without hard shadow; the base
  lighting shape of nearly every beauty close-up.
- **Beauty pass** — a slow camera glide across a face/hair/hand that lets light travel the surface
  as it moves, revealing luminosity and texture progressively rather than all at once.
- **Bloom (light)** — a soft flare of light that spreads from a bright source or reflective
  surface; used as a transition device only when it has a visible source in-frame (a "motivated"
  bloom), never as an unsourced effect.
- **Catchlight** — a small reflected highlight visible in an eye or on glass/liquid; a realism and
  liveliness cue when it traces to a real light source, a cheap "ring-light tell" when it doesn't.
- **Continuity shot / one-take** — a shot (or a sequence stitched to *read* as one shot via match
  transitions) with no visible cut, used to convey premium production control.
- **Cutaway** — a brief shot away from the main action (often an insert/macro) used to compress
  time or add texture without breaking the scene's continuity.
- **Dwell** — the beat of stillness held on a shot after its main action completes, letting the
  emotional or informational payload register before the next move.
- **Foley** — sound effects recorded/designed to match on-screen physical actions (a clink, a
  whoosh, a tick) rather than sourced from a stock library; luxury spots favor bespoke foley over
  generic stock SFX.
- **Graphic match** — a transition where the outgoing and incoming shots share a shape, color, or
  motion vector, making the cut read as continuous rather than edited.
- **Hero shot** — the shot in which the product is the unambiguous, fully legible subject, usually
  held longer and lit more carefully than any other frame.
- **Insert** — a close, often macro, shot of a detail (a droplet, a strand, a hand) inserted into
  a sequence to add texture, proof, or intimacy.
- **Jewel macro** — the treatment of a product (especially its liquid) as a gem: translucent,
  catching and holding light, photographed close enough that its material quality is the subject.
- **Kicker (light)** — a light placed to catch the edge of hair or a shoulder from behind/the side,
  separating the subject from the background and adding a rim of definition.
- **Match cut** — see graphic match; a cut disguised as continuity by matching form across the cut
  point.
- **Motivated light** — any light in the frame that can be traced to a plausible source in the
  world of the shot (a window, a lamp) as opposed to an unexplained, floating light.
- **Negative fill** — a dark surface placed to *remove* light/reflection from part of a subject,
  used to sculpt shadow and add dimensionality rather than flattening it with fill light.
- **Orbit (shot)** — a camera move that arcs around a static subject at a slow, even angular
  speed, typically used for a product hero reveal.
- **Practical (light source)** — a light source that is visible or implied to exist physically
  within the scene (a window, a lamp), as opposed to an off-camera rig light with no in-world
  source.
- **Rack focus** — a focus pull during a shot that shifts sharpness from one plane/subject to
  another, used to redirect attention without cutting.
- **Rim light** — light striking a subject from behind or the side, creating a bright edge that
  separates it from the background.
- **Shine ribbon** — a soft, moving band of specular highlight traveling along hair as it moves;
  the primary visual device for proving a shine claim.
- **Specular (highlight)** — a small, bright, mirror-like reflection of a light source on a glossy
  surface (skin, glass, liquid, metal); one clean specular reads as controlled lighting, several
  competing speculars read as over-lit.
- **Suspended highlight** — a point of light that appears to sit *inside* a translucent liquid's
  body, rather than only on its surface, signaling depth and richness.
- **Tabletop macro** — a close, controlled shot of a product or detail on a surface, typically with
  shallow depth of field isolating it from the background.
- **The turn** — a subject's rotation from away/profile toward the camera, timed to land a reveal
  on a specific beat of music, VO, or action.
- **Wraparound light** — a broad, soft key that envelops a face from multiple close angles,
  minimizing hard shadow; the classic "beauty light" shape.

## 9. Flagship cross-reference — beat-by-beat grammar map

Every beat of the locked flagship (Bible §6.1) mapped to the shot type(s) it uses (§2), the
reference-house code it borrows (§1), the macro-beauty vocabulary applied (§4), and the

      suspended highlight, etc.), not a generic "close-up."

      wash, never multiple competing highlights.

      static-present from the first frame of its beat.
