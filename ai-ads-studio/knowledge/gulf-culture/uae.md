## 2. UAE at a glance — the addressable-audience filter

Before any aesthetic decision, get the demographic reality right, because it changes who the ad
is actually for.

| Fact | Detail | Creative implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Population mix** | The UAE is an expatriate-majority country — Emirati nationals are a widely-cited **minority of roughly one-tenth to one-eighth** of total residents; the remainder is a large expatriate population led by South Asian communities (Indian, Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Filipino) alongside a substantial Arab-expat population (Egyptian, Levantine — Syrian/Lebanese/Jordanian/Palestinian — and other GCC nationals) and Western/other expats | This studio runs **Arabic-only** creative for UAE (`markets.yaml ae.language.note`: "KEEP the studio cut Arabic-MSA... English is out of scope for this framework"). The addressable audience for THIS ad is therefore **Arabic-fluent women** — Emirati nationals plus the Arab-expat community — not the full population. Do not brief casting, set, or copy as if targeting the broader multilingual population; South Asian and Western expat audiences are outside this creative's addressable reach until an English-language variant is separately commissioned (out of scope) |
| **Emirati citizens** | Higher household income on average, strong heritage pride, most brand-loyal to authenticity narratives, concentrated media consumption around national moments (Union Day, Ramadan) | The heritage story — **وصفة الجدات** (*waṣfat al-jaddāt*, "the grandmothers' recipe," Bible §3) — lands as authentic *and* aspirational with this segment; do not soften it into generic "natural beauty" language |
| **Arab-expat women (Egyptian, Levantine, other GCC)** | Large, price-and-value-aware, culturally fluent in MSA, often the largest practical share of Arabic-reading UAE social engagement | Heritage authenticity reads as nostalgic/trustworthy to this group too (many grew up with similar oil/herbal remedies at home); keep claims honest — this segment is retail-savvy and skeptical of over-promising (mirrors the Egypt dossier's caution, `markets.yaml eg.taboos`) |
| **Two-city reality** | Dubai and Abu Dhabi are the two commercial centers and read differently: **Dubai** = faster-paced, internationally exposed, higher tolerance for editorial/contemporary styling; **Abu Dhabi** = more formal, government-adjacent, slightly more conservative pacing and tone, though the same modesty floor applies to both | Default the cut to a "pan-UAE luxe" register that plays in both — avoid overtly Dubai-nightlife or overtly Abu-Dhabi-ceremonial signaling; the Bible §4 hero set (mihrab arch, travertine, hibiscus) is deliberately city-agnostic and is the safe default for both |
| **Currency anchor** | AED is pegged at a fixed rate to USD (3.6725 AED = 1 USD since 1997) — UAE consumers have decades of currency stability, unlike EGP's volatility (`markets.yaml eg`) | Price framing can lean on stable-value language (no "before inflation" hedging needed); never fabricate a converted AED number here — route to `07-offer-optimizer` (`markets.yaml ae.currency.price_policy`) |

**KSA contrast:** Saudi Arabia's population is majority-Saudi-national with a smaller expat share
proportionally concentrated in specific sectors; the addressable Arabic-speaking audience is
closer to the full population. UAE's addressable audience is a **narrower slice** of a
larger, more diverse population — plan reach expectations accordingly.

## 3. Aesthetic register — cosmopolitan Gulf luxe vs. Najdi-heritage luxe

| Axis | KSA (reference cut) | UAE (this dossier) |
|---|---|---|
| **Overall mood** | Heritage-forward, warm, Najdi-luxury — the flagship's Moroccan-arch hero set reads as an imported luxury motif inside a distinctly Saudi restraint | Cosmopolitan Gulf luxe — the **same** hero set (Bible §4 is market-agnostic) reads even more at-home here, because Dubai/Abu Dhabi luxury retail and hospitality design already lean heavily on Moorish-Andalusian arch motifs, brass, and travertine |
| **Reference points** | Riyadh five-star hospitality, Najdi majlis architecture, understated old-money restraint | Dubai/Abu Dhabi five-star hospitality (Burj Al Arab-adjacent design language, not the landmark itself — §7), international luxury retail (DIFC, The Dubai Mall high-end wing), contemporary Gulf design magazines |
| **Polish level** | Warm, intimate, slightly less "produced" — heritage authenticity is the selling point | Editorial-clean, higher production-value expectation — UAE audiences are more exposed to global DTC/beauty-brand advertising (Sephora-tier, international skincare) and benchmark against it; a cut that reads "home-video warm" in KSA can read "under-produced" in UAE if pushed too far. Keep the Bible §6 continuous-camera realism (never CGI-slick), but tighten grade, light control, and wardrobe finish |
| **Emotional lead** | Hope + restored femininity, heritage trust close behind (`markets.yaml sa.localization_notes`) | Same primary emotional spine (Bible §6) — hope + restored femininity — with heritage trust reading as **exotic-authentic** rather than **domestic-familiar** for a slice of the audience (Emirati) and **nostalgic-familiar** for another slice (Arab expat). Do not choose one over the other; the flagship's ambiguous pan-Arab warmth already serves both |
| **Pacing** | Warm and unhurried | Same unhurried luxury pacing (Bible §6.1's continuous camera move is universal), but the cosmopolitan competitive set rewards a slightly crisper cut-to-CTA — never rush the emotional beats, but do not let the offer beat feel like an afterthought against slicker competitors |

**Governing rule:** the aesthetic *floor* (modesty, product lock, negative-prompt core, §6.6) is
identical in both markets. What changes is **polish, latitude, and referential vocabulary** —
never the boundaries.

## 4. Modesty floor & fashion tolerance

The Bible §5 modesty floor — "elegant, modest, aspirational... beauty shown with restraint and
taste... never provocative" — is a **hard floor that does not move between markets**
(`markets.yaml shared.forbid_floor`). What moves is how much *contemporary/editorial* latitude
is available **within** that floor.

| Modesty dimension | KSA | UAE | What actually changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Modesty level label** | `very-high` (`markets.yaml sa.modesty_level`) | `high (cosmopolitan)` (`markets.yaml ae.modesty_level`) | UAE sits one notch below KSA on the studio's internal scale — still firmly modest, never approaching immodesty |
| **Silhouette** | Abaya-adjacent only | Abaya-adjacent **or** contemporary modest drape (tailored blazer-over-dress, structured modest separates) | UAE permits a tailored, editorial silhouette that would read slightly too "fashion-forward" for the strict KSA reference cut |
| **Fabric sheen/finish** | Matte crepe, silk, fine wool — deliberately non-glossy | Silk, crepe, satin — a touch more sheen/polish is acceptable | Satin's light-catching quality supports the "beauty pass" macro language (see `knowledge/luxury-beauty-ad-language.md` §4) slightly more richly here |
| **Jewelry** | Minimal — thin gold, a single ring | Delicate gold, "a slightly more elevated finish" (`markets.yaml ae.wardrobe_palette.jewelry`) | A fine layered necklace or a more detailed ring is acceptable in UAE; still nothing that competes with the bottle or the hair (Bible §6.1 governs frame hierarchy in both markets) |
| **Skin exposure** | Face, hands, hair only | Face, hands, hair only — **identical** | No loosening here in either market; "contemporary ≠ immodest" is explicit in `markets.yaml ae.taboos` |
| **Expression/gaze** | Calm, soft micro-smile, never coy | Same — calm-confident, soft micro-smile, never coy or wide/toothy (Output 4.3 pattern, `21-cultural-expert-gulf`) | No change — the TRANSFORMATION beat's expression direction is market-agnostic |

**The trap to avoid:** UAE's *wider society* is visibly more permissive than KSA's (tourism,
nightlife, mixed-gender public life are normal in Dubai/Abu Dhabi). **None of that bleeds into
this ad.** The brief's modesty floor is set by the product category (a heritage beauty ritual
aimed at a female audience) and the Bible, not by how permissive the surrounding city is. A
first-time UAE creative director on this account should assume the KSA reference cut's modesty
and only loosen the specific, named latitudes above — never infer additional latitude from
Dubai's general media environment.

## 5. Wardrobe palette & styling grammar

| Element | KSA (`markets.yaml sa.wardrobe_palette`) | UAE (`markets.yaml ae.wardrobe_palette`) | Styling note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base silhouette** | عباية (*ʿabāya*, "the loose over-garment") in warm neutrals | Abaya-adjacent OR contemporary modest drape, "a touch more polished-modern than KSA" | For UAE, a structured modest blazer-and-dress pairing or an elegantly draped modest gown reads as native luxury retail styling, not a departure from the brand |
| **Color palette** | Warm cream `#FAF6F1` · garnet red `#8E1B1E` · deep garnet `#6E1214` · gold `#C9A227` · soft brown `#6B564E` | Warm cream `#FAF6F1` · garnet red `#8E1B1E` · gold `#C9A227` · **champagne-neutral** · deep garnet `#6E1214` | UAE swaps in a champagne-neutral as a fifth tone — supports the brighter, more polished-luxe light register (§6) without leaving the brand's warm garnet/cream/gold DNA (Bible §3) |
| **Fabric** | Matte crepe, silk, fine wool drape; no logos, no loud print | Silk, crepe, satin drape; refined, editorial; still no immodest cut | Satin is UAE-appropriate where it would read slightly too glossy for the strict KSA cut |
| **Jewelry** | Minimal — thin gold, a single ring | Delicate gold; slightly more elevated finish | Keep it secondary to hair/bottle in every frame regardless of market |
| **Hair covering default** | Hair-visible default hero; hijab/styled-hair A/B always shipped | Identical — hair-visible default; hijab/styled-hair A/B always shipped (`markets.yaml ae.modest_alternative`) | No change: the Bible §5 mandatory A/B pair is universal, not market-specific |
| **No-logo rule** | Absolute — no visible brand logos on wardrobe | Absolute — same | UAE's luxury-goods-saturated environment makes this an *easier* trap to fall into (stylists reaching for recognizable pieces); enforce doubly hard here |

**Checklist for 04-creative-director / 09-beauty-commercial-director briefing a UAE wardrobe pull:**
- [ ] Silhouette is abaya-adjacent or a structured modest alternative — never a Western cocktail
      cut, never sleeveless, never a visible bust-line or leg above the ankle
- [ ] Palette stays inside the five UAE tones above; no cool/neutral grays or brand-off colors
- [ ] Fabric may include satin (unlike the strict KSA matte-only default)
- [ ] Jewelry is delicate and singular — one elevated piece, not a stack
- [ ] No visible logos, wordmarks, or recognizable luxury-house hardware
- [ ] Both A (hair-visible) and B (hijab) variants are pulled and briefed — never just the default

## 6. Interior style & environment vocabulary

The Bible §4 hero set — warm cream Moorish/mihrab arch, deep garnet drape, single red hibiscus,
palm frond, pale stone/travertine podium, soft directional window light — is explicitly
**market-agnostic** and ships identically to every market (`markets.yaml ae.interior_style.hero_set`:
"same brand hero set for product/offer beats — the lock is market-agnostic"). It in fact reads
*more* natively in UAE than anywhere else, because Dubai/Abu Dhabi hospitality and retail design
already lean on Moorish-Andalusian arches, brass fixtures, and travertine stone as a default luxury
vocabulary — this dossier's UAE-specific vocabulary is for the **broader scenes** around that hero
set, not the hero set itself.

| Environment element | KSA broader-scene vocabulary | UAE broader-scene vocabulary | Contrast note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Architectural register** | Najdi Gulf-luxury: brass, مشربية (*mashrabiyya*, a carved wood/lattice screen that filters light into geometric patterns) light, dates & hibiscus botanicals, Moroccan arch cues | Contemporary Gulf luxe: marble, brushed gold, floor-to-ceiling glazing/light, modern مجلس (*majlis*, "a formal sitting/reception room" — traditionally the space for hosting guests), desert-modern minimalism | UAE's register is brighter, more glazed/reflective, more "new luxury tower interior"; KSA's is warmer, more textile-and-brass "old luxury villa interior" |
| **Stone/surface material** | Pale stone/travertine podium (hero set, shared) | Same travertine podium, extended into broader-scene marble flooring/counters — travertine and marble read as the default UAE hospitality surface, so the hero set's podium blends seamlessly into a wider UAE interior without feeling imported | Travertine is genuinely *more* at-home visually in a UAE broader scene than in a Najdi one — use this as a strength, not a compromise |
| **Light quality** | Soft directional window light; warm; long gentle shadows; never flat studio glare | "Bright warm luxe; clean; still soft directional, never harsh CGI glare" (`markets.yaml ae.interior_style.light`) | UAE light can run brighter and cleaner (more glazing, more reflective marble) while staying strictly off the Bible §6.6 "HDR halos / CGI look" negative list |
| **Botanical accents** | Dates, red hibiscus, palm frond | Red hibiscus and palm frond (shared with the hero set), extendable with desert-modern minimalist greenery (a single architectural palm, never a busy floral arrangement) | Keep botanicals sparse — UAE's cleaner, less-cluttered contemporary aesthetic rewards restraint even more than KSA's already-restrained default |
| **Landmark avoidance (hard rule, both markets)** | No Kingdom Tower / Mecca / national-monument backdrops | **No Burj Khalifa, no Palm Jumeirah, no Sheikh Zayed Mosque skyline, no generic "Dubai tourism" establishing shots** | Using an identifiable landmark collapses "UAE" into "Dubai tourism postcard" and (a) misrepresents Abu Dhabi/other emirates, (b) reads as stock-footage cliché, a known cheap-ad tell (`knowledge/luxury-beauty-ad-language.md` and `10-luxury-commercial-director`'s cheap-ad-tell list), and (c) is entirely unnecessary — the brand's own hero set is a stronger, product-specific visual signature than any skyline |

## 7. Taboos & sensitivities (additive to the Bible §5 floor)

Per-market taboos **add** to `markets.yaml shared.forbid_floor`; they never remove from it
(`markets.yaml routing.hard_rules`). The floor itself — no alcohol cues, no immodesty, no
religious imagery as decoration, no fear-mongering about the body, no fake medical claims, no
pork/non-halal cues, no romantic/dating framing or intimate unrelated-gender free-mixing, no
disrespect to national symbols/flags/leadership — applies in UAE exactly as it does in KSA.

**UAE-specific additive taboos (`markets.yaml ae.taboos`, expanded):**

| Taboo | Why it matters here specifically | Practical direction |
|---|---|---|
| **No alcohol cues despite tourism visibility** | Dubai's tourism/nightlife imagery is globally visible (bars, beach clubs, brunches); a UAE creative team can be unconsciously primed by that surrounding media to let a glass, a bar silhouette, or a "resort" mood slip in | Audit every prop and background surface for anything glass-and-liquid that could misread as an alcohol cue, even accidentally (a garnet-oil pour near a stemmed glass, for instance) |
| **No disrespect to UAE leadership, the flag, or national symbols** | Same floor item as every market, but worth naming explicitly given how visible national-symbol imagery is in UAE retail/advertising generally (flags on buildings, ruler portraits in public spaces) — the brand must never appear to appropriate or casually reference these | Keep any national-moment creative (Union Day, §9) warm and generic-patriotic in tone, never using an actual flag graphic, emblem, or portrait as set dressing |
| **Avoid overt religious framing; heritage cues stay aesthetic, not devotional** | The Bible §4 hero set uses an arch silhouette drawn from Moorish-Andalusian design, not a devotional space — this reads cleanly in UAE's design-forward retail/hospitality context, but the same mihrab-arch clarification applies here as everywhere (see `21-cultural-expert-gulf` Output 4.4: it is a decorative architectural motif only — no qibla staging, no prayer rug, no Qur'anic calligraphy, no minbar, no call-to-prayer audio) | Route any set-dressing addition to `18-environment-realism-director` for the same religious-sensitivity check applied in every market — UAE gets no exemption and no extra scrutiny; the rule is identical |
| **Keep skin exposure to face/hands/hair; contemporary ≠ immodest** | The single most common failure mode when a UAE-savvy stylist over-reads "cosmopolitan" as license for more skin or a tighter cut | Enforce the §4 modesty table literally — "contemporary" in this brief means silhouette/fabric/jewelry latitude, never exposure latitude |
| **No unrelated men and women together — same floor as everywhere, but UAE has no KSA-level additive strictness on record** | KSA's per-market taboo list adds an explicit "no depiction of unrelated men & women together; keep the frame woman-centered & private" rule that goes beyond the shared floor. UAE's `markets.yaml` taboo list does not repeat that specific additive | In practice this is moot for the flagship (the beat sheet is a solo woman's private ritual in every market), but it means a UAE-market family/gathering cutdown (e.g., an Eid scene with a father or brother visible in soft background) has marginally more narrative room than a KSA equivalent would — confirm with `21-cultural-expert-gulf` before using that latitude, since the studio still defaults to the single primary cut model (Bible §8) |
| **Casting: avoid conflating "Emirati" with generic Gulf/Arab stereotypes** | Emirati dress and features are frequently flattened in outside media into a single "Gulf" look; the brand's default wardrobe is deliberately pan-Gulf-neutral (Bible §5) precisely to avoid this trap | Do not over-specify Emirati-only regalia (specific traditional embroidery, region-specific abaya cuts) unless the brief explicitly calls for an Emirati-heritage-specific cutdown — the standard flagship wardrobe already serves the market correctly without it |

## 8. Dialect & language register

**On-screen text is elegant MSA — فصحى (*fuṣḥā*, "the eloquent/standard register") — in every
market, with no exception** (`markets.yaml shared.language.on_screen_register`). This is
identical in KSA and UAE. The only place dialect enters is the **optional spoken VO** for
TikTok/Snap cutdowns (Bible §5, `markets.yaml shared.platform_vo_policy`).

| Register layer | KSA | UAE | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On-screen overlays** | MSA, western numerals, `139 ﷼` (Bible §6.3) | MSA, western numerals, AED price routed via `07-offer-optimizer` | Never re-translate the locked overlays (Bible §6.3) into dialect for either market — the overlay text is Bible-locked and market-agnostic in wording; only the price/currency token localizes |
| **Primary VO** | MSA (Bible §6.4) | MSA (identical five-line structure) | The primary cut's VO is the same script in every market; localization is the optional dialect layer below, not a rewrite of the primary VO |
| **Optional spoken VO dialect** | خليجي (*khalījī*, "Gulf") — Najdi/Hijazi register | خليجي (*khalījī*) — **Emirati** register specifically | Both markets use a Khaleeji VO option, but the specific regional flavor of Khaleeji differs — an Emirati voice talent does not sound identical to a Najdi one |

**A distinctive Emirati/Gulf dialect feature to brief a voice talent on (illustrative, VO-only —
never applied to on-screen MSA):** in traditional Emirati and wider Gulf speech, the feminine
second-person suffix (the "-ك" *-ki* ending, as in **شعركِ** *shaʿruki*, "your hair") is sometimes
realized with a "چ" (a "ch" sound) instead of "ك" — a recognized dialectal feature called
*kaskasa*. So the Bible's L1 hook line —

- **MSA (Bible §6.4, locked, on-screen and primary VO):**
  «شعركِ يتساقط… والسبب جذورٌ لا تصلها التغذية.»
  *shaʿruki yatasāqaṭ… wa-s-sababu judhūrun lā taṣiluhā at-taghdhiya*
  ("Your hair is falling… because the roots aren't getting nourishment.")

- **Illustrative Emirati-khaleeji spoken-VO texture (optional TikTok/Snap layer only, never
  overlay, never a replacement for the locked MSA line above):**
  «شعرچ يتساقط... والسبب إن الجذور ما توصلها التغذية.»
  *shaʿrach yatasāqaṭ... wa-s-sabab inn al-judhūr mā tawṣalha at-taghdhiya*
  (same gloss: "Your hair is falling… because the roots aren't getting nourishment.")

This is a **texture note for voice direction**, not a re-lock of Bible copy — the actual
production VO line must still be authored/approved by `20-arabic-copywriter` with
`21-cultural-expert-gulf` sign-off, and the *kaskasa* feature is not universal across all Emirati
speakers (it reads more traditional/Bedouin-Gulf than urban-cosmopolitan) — direct a voice talent
who produces it naturally rather than forcing an actor to perform it artificially, which reads as
inauthentic instantly to a native-Emirati ear.

**Hard rule carried from `markets.yaml ae.language.note`:** the UAE audience is genuinely
multilingual (Arabic + English is common in Dubai in particular), but **this framework's cut stays
Arabic-MSA only** for the beauty/heritage read — an English-language variant is out of scope and
must not be improvised into any UAE deliverable.

| **الصيف** (*aṣ-ṣayf*, "summer") | الصيف | June – August | Medium-high | Sun/chlorine/AC hair stress → repair/nourish angle; families travel, indoor ritual sells | Same core angle; UAE layers DSS on top (above) |
| **موسم الأعراس** (*mawsim al-aʿrās*, "wedding season") | موسم الأعراس | October – March (cooler months) | Medium | Henna-night tie-in — الحناء الطبيعية (*al-ḥinnāʾ aṭ-ṭabīʿiyya*, "natural henna," a locked ingredient, Bible §3) — bridal density & shine | Identical framing and window in KSA |

**Flight-planning takeaway for `03-marketing-strategist`:** UAE's promotional calendar is denser
in Q4–Q1 than KSA's — White Friday flows directly into DSF, creating a roughly seven-week
continuous high-intent commerce window (mid-November through end of January) that has no single
KSA equivalent of that length. Budget and creative-refresh cadence should account for this longer
sustained window rather than treating it as one spike.

## 10. Buying behavior & purchase psychology

| Dimension | KSA | UAE |
|---|---|---|
| **Price sensitivity** | Value-conscious; the −25% pack framing (`139 ﷼ بدل 185`, Bible §6.3) is a primary conversion lever | Less acutely price-sensitive on average (higher average household income, AED-USD peg stability) but still value-aware for a mass-beauty category product — the 3-pack-as-full-treatment framing (Bible §3 "the pack is the result") still outperforms single-bottle framing here |
| **Trust signals that convert** | COD + inspect-before-pay (فحص المنتج قبل الدفع, Bible §3) as the primary trust mechanic; verified-purchase reviews from named cities (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam) | COD remains a strong trust signal but is one of several — card/Apple-Pay-tier digital payment is itself a trust signal here (a UAE consumer often trusts a slick digital checkout as much as COD); reviews should reference Dubai/Abu Dhabi/Sharjah when a real UAE review corpus exists — **never fabricate a specific testimonial quote**; until a genuine UAE review corpus is collected, keep trust copy generic ("thousands of satisfied customers across the UAE") rather than inventing named reviewers or cities |
| **Competitive exposure** | Moderate exposure to international beauty-DTC advertising | High exposure — Dubai/Abu Dhabi consumers see global luxury and DTC beauty advertising constantly (in-mall, social, out-of-home); the creative bar for "looks premium enough to trust" is higher here, which is why §3's polish/production-value note matters commercially, not just aesthetically |
| **Heritage-authenticity appeal** | Reads as domestically authentic — "our grandmothers" is a shared cultural inheritance | Reads as **exotic-authentic** to Emirati viewers (Moroccan heritage as an admired outside tradition) and **nostalgic-familiar** to Arab-expat viewers (many grew up with comparable home herbal remedies); both readings support the same claim, so the flagship's ambiguous pan-Arab warmth needs no market-specific rewrite |
| **Channel behavior** | Meta/TikTok dominant; WhatsApp as a support/confirmation channel | Meta/TikTok/Snapchat all strong; WhatsApp equally central for order confirmation and customer support — UAE has some of the highest smartphone/social penetration in the region, supporting all three platforms at full weight (Bible §5 platform rules) |
| **Decision driver** | Solving a visible, felt problem (shedding, thinning) with a trusted heritage remedy at a fair, de-risked price | Same core driver — the product category (hair loss/thinning) is not price-driven by nature; the emotional hook (Bible §6's pain → heritage → transformation spine) carries the same weight here as in KSA. UAE buyers additionally reward a *frictionless* purchase experience — fast delivery (§12), flexible payment (§11), and a polished checkout are secondary but real conversion levers |

## 11. COD vs card — payment behavior deep dive

| Payment dimension | KSA (`markets.yaml sa.cod_norms`) | UAE (`markets.yaml ae.cod_norms`) |
|---|---|---|
| **COD dominance** | Very high — COD is the Bible-locked default and the primary trust mechanic; the offer is built around it (Bible §3) | High, but **not the single dominant path** — "COD widely expected, though card/digital penetration is higher than KSA" (`markets.yaml ae.cod_norms.dominance`) |
| **Card/digital-wallet penetration** | Lower — banking/telecom infrastructure and consumer habit favor COD as the trusted default for first-time e-commerce purchases | Meaningfully higher — UAE has strong card penetration and high adoption of digital wallets (Apple Pay/Google Pay-tier tap-to-pay), plus growing use of buy-now-pay-later services; a UAE shopper is comfortable completing a card checkout for a first-time purchase in a way a KSA shopper is often not |
| **Offer presentation** | Lead exclusively with «الدفع عند الاستلام» (*ad-dafʿ ʿind al-istilām*, "cash on delivery / payment on receipt") as the CTA's trust anchor (Bible §6.3) | Present **both** options — lead with COD as the trust anchor (it still out-converts as a first-impression trust signal, `markets.yaml ae.cod_norms.confirmation`: "offer both COD and prepaid, lead with COD trust") but do not present COD as the only path; a UAE landing/checkout experience that hides the card option looks behind the market, not premium |
| **COD fees** | Not a notable friction point at the offer-copy level | Some UAE couriers add a small COD surcharge — keep the on-frame "3-pack + free shipping" value message clean and untouched by this; it is a checkout-flow detail for `07-offer-optimizer`/fulfillment, never something the ad copy needs to caveat |
| **Confirmation call** | Standard phone confirmation before dispatch | Phone/WhatsApp confirmation is common and expected; less operationally critical than in Egypt's high-refusal-risk environment (`markets.yaml eg.cod_norms.risk_note`), but still standard practice |
| **Inspect-before-pay** | A headline trust element — فحص المنتج قبل الدفع (*faḥṣ al-muntaj qabl al-dafʿ*, "inspect the product before paying") | Carries over identically as a trust mechanic when COD is chosen (`markets.yaml ae.cod_norms.inspect_before_pay: true`) |
| **Creative implication** | The locked flagship CTA («اطلبي الآن — الدفع عند الاستلام») ships as-is — COD is the entire payment story | The same CTA line ships as-is (Bible §6.3 wording is not re-translated per-market), but the **landing/checkout experience** behind the ad (owned by `07-offer-optimizer` and fulfillment, not by the video creative itself) should surface card/digital payment as a visible, equally legitimate option rather than COD-only |

## 12. Delivery expectations by emirate

`markets.yaml ae.delivery_expectation` gives the summary; UAE delivery infrastructure is
meaningfully faster and more granular by emirate than KSA's nationwide window, which is worth
detailing for offer-copy and customer-expectation-setting purposes.

| Emirate / area | Typical delivery window | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Dubai** | Same-day to next-day | Fastest fulfillment infrastructure in the market; sets the benchmark UAE-wide consumer expectation for speed |
| **Abu Dhabi** | Same-day to next-day | Comparable to Dubai for major fulfillment networks |
| **Sharjah** | Same-day to next-day | Effectively part of the Dubai metro logistics footprint |
| **Ajman, Umm Al Quwain, Ras Al Khaimah, Fujairah (the Northern Emirates)** | 1–3 business days | Slightly longer than the three major hubs but still fast relative to KSA's 24–48 h major-city / 1–4-day nationwide window (Bible §3) |

**Framing rule:** the Bible only Bible-locks a delivery promise for KSA (24–48 h major cities,
1–4 business days nationwide, Bible §3 — this exact number is never applied to UAE copy). UAE
delivery-window claims are **market expectations**, not Bible-locked promises
(`markets.yaml ae.delivery_expectation.note`) — confirm any on-frame delivery-speed claim with
fulfillment before it ships, and never borrow the KSA number for a UAE cut.

## 13. Localization checklist for the creative team

- [ ] **Currency:** AED (`د.إ`), price sourced from `07-offer-optimizer` — never a converted 139
      SAR figure written directly into this document or into copy
- [ ] **On-screen register:** elegant MSA, western numerals — identical wording to the Bible §6.3
      locked overlays except the price/currency token
- [ ] **Primary VO:** MSA, same five-line structure as Bible §6.4 — no dialect on the primary track
- [ ] **Optional dialect VO:** Emirati khaleeji register for TikTok/Snap only, directed with a
      native Emirati voice talent (§8) — never forced onto a non-native performer
- [ ] **Wardrobe:** abaya-adjacent or contemporary modest drape, UAE five-tone palette (§5),
      satin permitted, delicate-elevated jewelry, zero visible logos
- [ ] **Interior:** contemporary Gulf luxe broader scenes (marble, brushed gold, modern majlis) —
      hero set (Bible §4) ships unchanged
- [ ] **No landmark shots:** no Burj Khalifa, no Palm Jumeirah, no identifiable skyline (§6)
- [ ] **Payment framing:** COD leads, card/digital payment visibly available — never COD-only in
      the checkout/landing experience behind the ad (§11)
- [ ] **Delivery claims:** UAE-specific windows only (§12), never the KSA-locked number
- [ ] **A/B pair shipped:** hair-visible default + hijab/styled-hair alternative, both at full
      production fidelity (Bible §5, identical requirement to every market)
- [ ] **Seasonality:** flight plan checked against §9, including the UAE-only DSF/DSS windows that
      have no KSA equivalent
- [ ] **Cultural sign-off:** routed through `21-cultural-expert-gulf` against the additive taboo
      list in §7 before Stage 10 closes
