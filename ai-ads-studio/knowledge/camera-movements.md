## 1. Scope, authority & how this catalog is used

This document is the studio's **general camera-movement library** — every physical camera
technique this framework is willing to reach for, independent of any one ad. It sits one level
above `12-camera-director`'s per-ad output exactly the way `knowledge/transition-library.md`
sits one level above `15-transition-designer`'s five selected engines:

- **This catalog** answers *"what camera moves exist in the studio's vocabulary, and what is
  each one for?"* — ten entries, each with a definition, its rig, its motivation categories, its
  lens/speed defaults, and **ready-to-paste prompt phrasing** for `26-kie-prompt-builder`.
- **`12-camera-director`'s Output 3.1** answers *"which of these does THIS ad sanction, and
  exactly how?"* — for the flagship it locks **seven** of this catalog's ten as its per-beat
  lexicon (slow push, slow pull, orbit, slider, macro dive, focus pull, tasteful whip), while two
  more entries here — **crane** and **intentional handheld** — describe the *rig/technique*
  underneath specific locked moves (the B2 pull is executed on a crane; the B4 push is executed
  with an intentional-handheld feel), and the tenth — **speed ramp** — is a *timing* property
  layered on top of any move, not a move in itself.
- **Where this document and `12-camera-director`'s locked lexicon differ in scope, the locked
  lexicon governs the flagship render.** This catalog is the wider shelf the studio draws from
  for every future product; §8 below shows exactly how the flagship draws from it.

**Format defaults every move in this catalog assumes** (Bible §5): **9:16** vertical, **24–30
fps** with a **24 fps** priority, **180° shutter** (1/48 s at 24 fps) for natural filmed
motion-blur, **physical moves only** — dolly, jib/crane, slider, orbit, macro push — **never an
optical zoom**, safe margins **top ~14% / bottom ~20%** kept clear of overlays, and every move
motivated by something in the story with a named governor that stops it (never random drift,
never energy-for-its-own-sake).

**The flagship reference:** every "Flagship usage" line below cites **«القطرة التي تُعيد
الحياة»** (*al-qaṭra allatī tuʿīd al-ḥayāt*, "The Drop That Brings Hair Back to Life," codename
**UNBROKEN THREAD**, Bible §6) for **زيت المشاط الأحمر** (*zayt al-mishāṭ al-aḥmar*, "Red Mechat
Oil," Bible §3) — the studio's one locked, fully-worked example. The product it must never distort
is the exact §4 lock: clear rounded-rectangular ~250 ml PET bottle, **matte white ribbed screw
cap** (never gold/black), **translucent deep garnet/ruby-red oil** (never orange/brown/pink),
white/red label reading **«زيت المشاط»** (*zayt al-mishāṭ*, "Mechat Oil," largest) over **«الأحمر
للشعر»** (*al-aḥmar lil-shaʿr*, "red, for hair"), with a gold **«طبيعي 100٪»** (*ṭabīʿī miʾa
bi-l-miʾa*, "100% natural") seal.

## 3. The move catalog

### 3.1 Push-in (slow physical dolly-in)

**Definition.** The camera physically travels toward the subject along the lens axis, on a
dolly, slider, or motion-control rig. It is **never** an optical zoom — a push changes
perspective naturally as the camera advances; a zoom flattens it and reads as cheap (§6, R3
below).

**When/why.** Reach for a push-in when the story wants the audience physically **closer** to
something — rising intimacy on a pain detail, a face turning to meet the lens, a product worth
settling on. Never use it to manufacture "energy" with no destination.

**Lens/DoF.** Matched to subject scale: **~100 mm macro, very shallow DoF** for a texture/product
detail; **~50–85 mm portrait, shallow with eyes/label tack-sharp** for a face or settled product.

**Speed.** Near-imperceptible on a macro hook (the stillness *is* the point); an eased S-curve on
a portrait push, settling to a governed hold. A push-in is the one move that may carry the
studio's single sanctioned time-remap (§3.9) if the beat calls for it.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:**
- *Macro/product version:* "an almost imperceptible physical push-in on a locked macro slider,
  roughly one to two centimeters of travel across the shot, 100 mm macro lens, very shallow depth
  of field, 180-degree shutter, zero handheld jitter, easing smoothly with no constant-velocity
  drift."
- *Portrait version:* "a slow physical dolly-in on an operated gimbal, arcing gently toward her
  over-the-shoulder as she turns to meet the lens, 50 to 85 millimeter portrait lens, eye-level,
  shallow depth of field holding her eyes tack-sharp, easing into a settled hold, a trace of
  operated human movement, never mechanically constant."

**Flagship usage.** **B1 HOOK** (0.0–1.2 s) — a near-locked macro push on the comb as the strand
slips off the teeth, carrying the single time-remap into slow-motion. **B4 TRANSFORMATION**
(5.2–6.6 s) — a slow push on a slight over-the-shoulder arc-in to eye contact, executed with the
intentional-handheld technique (§3.10). Bible §6.1.

**Failure tell + fix.** A snap-in or zoom-punch is a "cheap-ad" tell — always a physical move
(never optical); a push with no named stopping point drifts past a locked-off frame — state the
governor before you write the move.

### 3.2 Pull-back (slow physical dolly-out / reveal)

**Definition.** The camera physically travels away from, or rises up and out of, a subject,
disclosing more of the world than was visible a moment before.

**When/why.** Reach for a pull-back specifically for a **reveal** — the story is withholding
something (a hero, a set, an answer) and the pull is the mechanism that discloses it. A pull with
nothing new revealed at its end is aimless drift, not a reveal.

**Lens/DoF.** Opens on a wider reveal lens (~35–50 mm) and can ease narrower as the disclosed
subject is found and settled on.

**Speed.** Real-time, **easing out to a held frame** — a governor the reveal rests on rather than
sailing past.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "a slow physical pull-back and rise on a stabilized jib,
surfacing up and out of the oil surface toward the product, 35 to 50 millimeter lens opening the
frame as the translucent garnet-red bottle comes into view, easing out to a settled, locked hero
frame with the label square to camera, no drift past the hold."

**Flagship usage.** **B2 DISCOVERY** (1.6–3.0 s) — pulls up out of the oil surface to reveal the
Mechat bottle in warm Moorish-arch light (Bible §4 hero set), then settles into a push-and-rack
onto the label (§3.1/§3.7 compound). Bible §6.1.

**Failure tell + fix.** A pull-back that reveals nothing (empty frame expanding) reads as
unmotivated float — always name what the pull discloses, and lock a hold the instant it lands.

### 3.3 Orbit (slow arc around a static hero)

**Definition.** The camera arcs around a stationary subject at a slow, even angular velocity,
along a circular or partial-circular path — the product's "in the round" moment.

**When/why.** Reach for an orbit when a hero (almost always the product) has **earned** the right
to be seen from multiple angles without a cut — a resolve, not a decoration. Not for anything that
moves or acts; that calls for a slider (§3.4) instead.

**Lens/DoF.** ~35–50 mm, shallow DoF but with the **label plane held sharp throughout the arc** —
an orbit that lets the label soften has failed its one job.

**Speed.** **One calm, unhurried arc** — never a spinning showroom turntable, and never a full
360° when the story only needs a partial sweep.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "a slow, even, motion-control orbit arcing around the bottle
at near-eye-level, the translucent garnet-red oil catching one clean specular highlight as the
arc turns, the matte white ribbed cap and the white-and-red label with «زيت المشاط» staying
perfectly sharp and unwarped through the full arc, mid-arc two additional identical bottles rise
smoothly into frame to complete the three-bottle pack, the camera easing to a stop on the resolved
hero pack."

**Flagship usage.** **B5 OFFER+CTA** (7.0–8.0 s) — orbits the single bottle; mid-orbit two more
bottles rise into place, resolving on the **3-pack** offer (**139 SAR** instead of 185, free
shipping, **الدفع عند الاستلام** — *ad-dafʿ ʿind al-istilām*, "cash on delivery," Bible §3/§6.1).

**Failure tell + fix.** A fast or dizzy orbit reads as a "spinny showroom turntable" tell (a
`10-luxury-commercial-director` §3.7 cheap-ad tell); keep the arc slow and singular, and budget
only the angular distance the story needs inside an 8 s runtime.

### 3.4 Slider / truck (lateral track parallel to the action)

**Definition.** A linear, lateral camera move on a slider or truck dolly — the camera travels
**sideways relative to the subject**, tracking parallel to a line of motion, rather than toward
or away from it.

**When/why.** Reach for a slider when the action itself is traveling along a line — a part-line,
a strand's length, a gesture moving sideways — and the camera's job is to stay locked to that
axis, following rather than approaching.

**Lens/DoF.** ~100 mm macro, very shallow, so oil sheen and strand highlights read tactile, not
clinical.

**Speed.** Gentle, unhurried, real-time — the ritual **is** the proof; a camera in a hurry
undercuts it. Focus **follows** the moving action continuously rather than racking (§3.7).

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "a smooth lateral macro track on a probe slider, following
the fingertips along the part-line as they massage the garnet-tinted oil into the roots, then
gliding down the length of a section of dark, healthy hair, 100 millimeter macro lens, very
shallow depth of field, focus continuously following the fingertips and the oil sheen, no
rack, no jitter, calm and unhurried."

**Flagship usage.** **B3 RITUAL** (3.4–4.8 s) — tracks along the part-line as fingertips work the
oil in, then glides down a section of hair; hands off into the T3 hair-sweep whip (§3.8). Bible
§6.1.

**Failure tell + fix.** A lateral drift with nothing to follow reads as unmotivated float; a
slider must always be locked to a physical line of action already present in the frame.

### 3.5 Crane (vertical boom rise/fall)

**Definition.** A jib/boom move along the **vertical** axis — descending or ascending — distinct
from a lateral slider (§3.4) or an axial push/pull (§3.1/§3.2). A crane is what physically
executes a continuous down-then-up throughline without a cut.

**When/why.** Reach for a crane when the story needs the camera to **follow something falling or
rising through vertical space** — a drop's descent, a bottle's discovery surfacing into frame —
and needs that vertical journey to read as one uninterrupted move rather than two separate
setups stitched together.

**Lens/DoF.** ~100 mm macro on the descent (following the drop at macro scale); widens toward
~35–50 mm as the boom rises into the wider DISCOVERY reveal.

**Speed.** Real-time, **momentum-carried across the seam** — the descent's downward velocity is
the same velocity the rise begins from, so the down-and-up reads as one physical arc, not a reset.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "a continuous crane move that follows the falling translucent
garnet oil drop straight down into the oil surface, and without cutting rises back up and out of
the oil to reveal the bottle in warm Moorish-arch light, momentum carried smoothly through the
ripple with no velocity jump, motion-control smooth, zero jitter."

**Flagship usage.** The vertical spine that carries **T1 → B2** (1.2–3.0 s): the macro dive
(§3.6) following the drop down IS a crane-down; the pull-back (§3.2) revealing the bottle IS a
crane-up on the same boom. This is the rig behind Bible §6.1's transition-OUT/IN pair and matches
`15-transition-designer`'s "continuous crane down-and-up" support move and `12-camera-director`'s
B2 rig note ("jib/crane pull on a stabilized head").

**Failure tell + fix.** A crane move that resets its height between the down and the up (a hidden
cut disguised as continuity) breaks the one-take; the descent and the rise must share one
unbroken momentum curve, never a reset.

### 3.6 Macro dive (physical push into a surface at macro scale)

**Definition.** A physical push of the lens **into** a surface at macro scale — a real macro lens
genuinely nearing and passing through the near-focus plane of a liquid, a pour, or a texture.
This is **not** a CGI "fly-through" — every macro dive in this catalog must be reproducible by a
real macro probe lens.

**When/why.** Reach for a macro dive when the story needs the camera to physically **enter** a
surface — the studio's core transition-engine technique: a drop meeting an oil surface, a pour
being followed down.

**Lens/DoF.** ~100 mm macro, very shallow, product-level distance (1–2 cm from the surface).

**Speed.** Real-time with a slight accelerate into the dive; ramps back toward real-time on
emergence on the far side.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "a physical macro push directly into the falling ribbon of
translucent garnet-red oil as it pours, the lens diving through the liquid until warm garnet and
gold light fill the frame completely, then emerging on the far side onto the scalp in warm side
light, no optical zoom, no CGI fly-through artifact, a clean laminar liquid ribbon with no
banding."

**Flagship usage.** **T1** (1.2–1.6 s) — follows the falling oil drop down into the oil surface.
**T2** (3.0–3.4 s) — dives into the pour as the bottle tilts. These are the two seams the Bible
names explicitly: *"the oil drop and the hair strand ARE the transition engines"* (§6).

**Failure tell + fix.** A fake CGI "fly-through" or a morphing plunge is a synthetic-transition
tell; always render the dive as a physical push a real macro lens could produce, never a graphic
wipe or a deforming morph.

### 3.7 Focus pull (rack focus / follow focus)

**Definition.** A directed shift of the focal plane within one continuous shot — substituting for
a cut by moving *what the lens resolves sharply*, not where the camera physically sits.

**When/why.** Reach for a focus pull when the **story changes what matters** in an unmoving or
already-moving frame: a rising silhouette resolves into a legible label; a face becomes the point
after a hand exits frame. There are exactly three sanctioned behaviors and one forbidden one:

| Behavior | What it is | Sanctioned? |
|----------|-----------|:---:|
| **Rack** | A deliberate, motivated shift from one named plane to another | ✅ |
| **Follow** | Continuous tracking focus on a moving subject (never snaps) | ✅ |
| **Hold** | Focus locks and does not move at all | ✅ |
| **Hunt** | Focus searches back and forth for a target | ❌ vetoed |
| **Breathe** | Focus drifts subtly even on a declared hold | ❌ vetoed |

**Lens/DoF.** Shallow DoF is what makes the rack visible and expressive; the target plane always
corresponds to what the story needs sharp — the label, the eyes, the fingertips.

**Speed.** A rack is a single deliberate move with a named start and end; a follow tracks
continuously; a hold is locked and does not breathe.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:**
- *Rack:* "a directed focus rack from the rising bottle's silhouette to the front label as it
  squares to camera, landing crisp and holding — «زيت المشاط» legible — no focus hunting, no
  breathing on the hold."
- *Follow:* "focus continuously follows the fingertips and the oil sheen as they move down the
  section of hair, never hunting, never snapping to a new target."

**Flagship usage.** **B2** (1.6–3.0 s) — racks from the rising bottle silhouette to the label.
**B3** (3.4–4.8 s) — continuous follow-focus on the fingertips and oil line. **B4** (5.2–6.6 s) —
racks and lands on her eyes, then holds through a real blink. Bible §6.1.

**Failure tell + fix.** Focus hunting or focus breathing on a hold is one of the fastest tells of
a generated clip; every rack must be written with a named start plane, a named end plane, and an
explicit hold, never left to "find" its target.

### 3.8 Whip (tasteful whip / motion-blurred pivot)

**Definition.** A short, motion-blurred pivot of the camera **riding a physical motion already
present in the frame** — hair sweeping, a gesture crossing — never a glitch-whip preset, a
snap-zoom, or a whip inserted purely for style.

**When/why.** Reach for a whip only at a **transition seam**, and only when there is already a
physical motion in the world for the camera to ride. A whip with no motion to follow is a
decoration, not a transition.

**Lens/DoF.** Matches the surrounding beat's lens (macro-in, portrait-out for the flagship's
single whip); the motion-blur through the moving subject **is** the visual content of the whip.

**Speed.** Short — under 0.4 s — a fast blur peak that decelerates and clears into the next beat's
opening frame.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "a short, tasteful, motion-blurred camera whip riding the
exact direction of the hand sweeping a section of oiled hair across the lens, natural motion-blur
through the moving strands, decelerating and clearing as the woman's over-the-shoulder turn
begins, no glitch preset, no snap-zoom, no jump."

**Flagship usage.** **T3** (4.8–5.2 s) — rides the hair-sweep as RITUAL hands off into
TRANSFORMATION; the sole whip in the entire 8 s primary cut. Bible §6.1.

**Failure tell + fix.** "Whip-for-its-own-sake" and glitch/whoosh-swipe transition presets are
explicit anti-drift tells (Bible §6.6); a whip must always ride a physical motion already present
in the frame — never be inserted as a stylistic flourish with nothing to follow.

### 3.9 Speed ramp (time-remap + move-velocity S-curve — two distinct techniques)

**Definition.** Two different things share the word "ramp," and they must never be confused:

| Technique | What it changes | Where it's used |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| **Time-remap** (true slow-motion) | The footage's own **time** — the world itself slows down | Exactly **once** per creative |
| **Move-velocity S-curve** | The camera rig's physical **travel speed** eases in and out — a *velocity* shape, not a *time* shape | **Every** move, every beat |

**When/why — time-remap.** Reserved for exactly one emotionally loaded beat per the studio's
luxury-pacing law (`10-luxury-commercial-director` §3.4): blanket-applying slow-motion turns
filmed footage into a music-video montage and is a direct §6.6 negative risk. **When/why —
S-curve.** Applied to **every single move** in the catalog above; a move that starts or stops at
constant velocity is one of the fastest tells of a CGI/robotic render.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:**
- *Time-remap:* "the strand releases from the comb and falls in cinematic slow-motion, ramped to
  roughly half speed, then ramps back up to real-time as the camera follows the drop down — the
  single slow-motion moment in the film."
- *S-curve:* "the camera move eases in gently from rest and eases out to a smooth, settled stop —
  no constant-velocity travel at any point, no mechanical start or stop."

**Flagship usage.** **Time-remap** is used **exactly once** — the falling strand in **B1**
(0.0–1.2 s), ramping to ~50% and ramping back to real-time across **T1** (1.2–1.6 s). Every other
beat, **including B4 the keeper**, is emphatically real-time so the blink and breath read as a
real person, never a stylized montage. **Move-velocity S-curves** apply to every beat and every
transition without exception — B2's ease-out to a held hero frame, B4's ease to a breathing hold,
B5's ease to a stop on the resolved 3-pack, all four transition seams' momentum-carry. Bible §6.1;
`12-camera-director` R4/Output 3.5.

**Failure tell + fix.** A second slow-motion anywhere else in the film — most dangerously at B4,
the keeper — is an automatic veto: it breaks the "real person, not montage" read the whole
transformation beat depends on. If a render shows any beat besides B1 in slow-motion, or shows
constant-velocity travel on any move, it fails the gate and is re-rendered with the S-curve made
explicit in the prompt.

### 3.10 Intentional handheld (operated trace-of-life — the ONE sanctioned exception)

**Definition.** A deliberately "operated" camera feel — gimbal or Steadicam-held rather than
locked motion-control — that retains a trace of natural human micro-movement **without** crossing
into shake, wobble, or jitter. This is the single, narrow exception to the rule that every other
move in the ad is maximally stabilized motion-control.

**This is not the same thing as, and must never be confused with, shaky-cam or
handheld-wobble-for-energy** — both of those remain hard vetoes everywhere in this framework,
including on the one beat that uses intentional handheld. The difference is control: intentional
handheld is *operated by a master*, invisible in its stabilization, present only as a trace of
life; shaky-cam is *visibly unstable* and reads as amateur or broken footage.

**When/why.** Reserved for exactly one situation in this framework: a **face-move** where a
perfectly robotic, mechanically smooth arc would itself read as CGI. A human face turning to meet
a lens is the one place absolute mechanical perfection becomes a tell rather than a virtue.
Everywhere else in the ad — every macro, every product beat, the orbit — maximal motion-control
stabilization is correct and this technique is **not** used.

**Lens/DoF.** ~50–85 mm portrait, shallow DoF, **eyes tack-sharp** even through the operated
movement.

**Speed.** Real-time, easing to a breathing hold — a real blink and a real breath must read as
human on this move; that is the entire reason the technique exists.

**Ready-to-paste prompt phrasing:** "an operated, gimbal-held camera feel — handheld-by-a-master,
not shaky-cam — arcing gently over her shoulder as she turns to meet the lens, stabilized enough
that there is zero visible jitter, human enough that the move doesn't feel mechanically perfect,
easing into a settled hold where a natural blink and a soft breath read as completely real."

**Flagship usage.** **B4 TRANSFORMATION** (5.2–6.6 s) — the keeper — is the **sole** beat in the
entire 8 s primary cut that uses this technique. Every other beat and every transition uses
maximal motion-control stabilization instead (§3.5–§3.9 above; Bible §6.1; `12-camera-director`
R5/Output 3.6).

**Failure tell + fix.** This is the narrowest, most misuse-prone entry in the catalog.
"Intentional handheld" must never degrade into the vetoed tells `handheld wobble-for-"energy"` or
`shaky-cam` (Bible §6.6). If a generation shows visible frame shake anywhere **outside** B4, or
shows shake at B4 that reads as amateur rather than deliberately operated, it fails the gate and
must be re-rendered with tighter stabilization language layered back in.

## 4. Compound moves — how the flagship chains catalog entries per beat

No beat in the flagship uses exactly one catalog entry in isolation; each beat is a **small,
motivated sequence** of entries from §3, always resolving to a named governor. This is the pattern
`26-kie-prompt-builder` compiles into continuous prose, and the pattern any new product's camera
plan should follow.

| Beat / seam | t (s) | Catalog entries combined (in order) | The governor that stops the sequence |
|-------------|-------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **B1 HOOK** | 0.0–1.2 | Push-in (§3.1) + time-remap (§3.9) | The strand releases; the ramp carries into T1 |
| **T1** | 1.2–1.6 | Crane-down (§3.5) + macro dive (§3.6) + time-remap tail (§3.9) | The drop lands; ripple carries into B2 |
| **B2 DISCOVERY** | 1.6–3.0 | Crane-up / pull-back (§3.2/§3.5) + push-settle (§3.1) + focus rack (§3.7) | Held hero frame on the squared label |
| **T2** | 3.0–3.4 | Macro dive (§3.6) | Emerges onto the scalp for B3 |
| **B3 RITUAL** | 3.4–4.8 | Slider/truck (§3.4) + follow-focus (§3.7) | The hair sweeps across the lens (hands off to T3) |
| **T3** | 4.8–5.2 | Whip (§3.8) | Clears into her turn |
| **B4 TRANSFORMATION** | 5.2–6.6 | Push-in (§3.1) + intentional handheld (§3.10) + focus rack (§3.7) + S-curve hold (§3.9) | Eye contact + the breathing hold |
| **T4** | 6.6–7.0 | S-curve deceleration only (§3.9); no camera move library entry — the beat is carried by light (bloom), not camera travel | Bloom recedes onto the product |
| **B5 OFFER+CTA** | 7.0–8.0 | Orbit (§3.3) + focus hold (§3.7) + S-curve stop (§3.9) | Resolve on the crisp 3-pack hero frame |

**Reading the table top to bottom traces the whole unbroken path**: push → crane-down/dive →
crane-up/pull/push/rack → dive → slide → whip → push/handheld/rack → settle → orbit. No entry
appears out of its motivated place, and every sequence ends on a named governor — the discipline
that keeps the 8 s reading as one continuous camera path rather than nine stitched shots (§8).

## 5. Rig & equipment glossary

| Term | What it is | Primary catalog entries it executes |
|------|-----------|--------------------------------------|
| **Dolly** | A wheeled or track-mounted camera platform for smooth axial travel | Push-in (§3.1), pull-back (§3.2) |
| **Slider** | A short rail system for precise, repeatable linear travel over 0.3–2 m | Slider/truck (§3.4), macro push (§3.1 macro variant) |
| **Jib / crane** | A counterweighted boom arm giving vertical (and some lateral) travel from a fixed base | Pull-back (§3.2), crane (§3.5) |
| **Gimbal** | A motorized 3-axis stabilizer worn or handheld, absorbing operator movement while preserving intentional motion | Whip (§3.8), intentional handheld (§3.10) |
| **Steadicam** | A body-worn mechanical stabilizer rig; the classic "operated but smooth" feel | Intentional handheld (§3.10) |
| **Motion control (MoCo)** | A programmable, robot-repeatable rig that executes an identical move on every take | Orbit (§3.3), crane (§3.5), macro dive (§3.6) |
| **Macro probe lens / macro probe slider** | A long, narrow macro lens on a slider, built for extreme close focus at product/texture scale | Macro dive (§3.6), slider/truck (§3.4) |
| **Follow-focus** | A geared or motorized control for precise, repeatable focal-plane changes during a shot | Focus pull (§3.7), on any rig above |
| **Stabilized head** | A gyro-damped mount atop a jib/crane/dolly that removes residual vibration | Pull-back (§3.2), crane (§3.5), orbit (§3.3) |

## 6. Universal rules that govern every move in this catalog

These ten rules apply to **every** entry in §3, regardless of which product's ad is being built.
They are condensed from `12-camera-director`'s R1–R9 and the Bible's §4–§7 locks; the full
authoritative language lives in that skill.

1. **Physical, never optical.** Every move is a real dolly/jib/slider/orbit/macro-push; zoom,
   zoom-punch, and snap-zoom are vetoed everywhere (§3.1 R3).
2. **180° shutter (1/48 s at 24 fps) by default.** The natural-motion-blur look is a large part
   of why a move reads as filmed rather than rendered.
3. **Ease-in/ease-out (S-curve) on every move, no exceptions.** Constant-velocity travel is a
   robotic/CGI tell (§3.9).
4. **Exactly one time-remap (slow-motion) per creative.** Placed on the single most emotive
   frame; everywhere else — including any keeper/transformation beat — stays real-time (§3.9).
5. **Every move needs a named motivation and a named governor.** State, in one sentence, *why*
   the camera moves and *what stops it*; a move that fails this test is drift, not direction.
6. **Motion-control everywhere except the one deliberate face-move.** Maximal stabilization is
   the default; intentional handheld (§3.10) is the sole, narrow exception — never spread it.
7. **The product lock always wins over the shot.** Any move near the bottle stays slow and
   square-to-label; a "cooler" angle that risks a warped label or a color-shifted liquid is
   rejected outright (Bible §4).
8. **Frame with dignity.** Eye-level on any face-move — never a high-angle looking down on the
   model (Bible §5).
9. **Focus is directed, never hunted, never breathing on a hold** (§3.7).
10. **Momentum carries across every transition seam.** The camera's exit velocity at the end of
    one beat is the entry velocity of the next — no cut, no teleport, no velocity jump (§4, §8).

## 7. Anti-pattern checklist — vetoed camera clichés and their fix

| Vetoed cliché | Why it fails | Use instead |
|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| Zoom-punch / snap-zoom | Optical zoom flattens perspective; reads as cheap/edited | A physical push-in (§3.1) or pull-back (§3.2) |
| Shaky-cam / handheld-wobble-for-energy | Reads as amateur or broken footage, not "energy" | Motion-control stabilization; intentional handheld (§3.10) only at the one designated face-move |
| Spinny showroom turntable | A fast orbit reads as a stock product-shot cliché | A slow, single, calm orbit (§3.3) |
| Random camera drift / aimless push-in | No motivation, no governor — reads as AI wander | Every move must name its motivation and its stopping point (§6 rule 5) |
| Glitch-whip / whoosh-swipe preset | A stylized digital transition preset, not a physical camera move | A tasteful whip riding an existing physical motion (§3.8) |
| CGI "fly-through" plunge | Physically impossible for a real lens; reads as synthetic | A physical macro-dive push into the surface (§3.6) |
| Second slow-motion | Turns filmed footage into a music-video montage | Real-time everywhere except the single designated beat (§3.9) |
| Focus hunting / focus breathing on a hold | A dead giveaway of a generated clip | Direct every rack/follow/hold explicitly (§3.7) |
| Fast push/whip/orbit near the product | Risks a warped label or a morphing bottle | Slow, square, motion-control moves near any product beat (§6 rule 7) |
| High-angle looking down on the model | Breaks the dignity/modesty floor | Eye-level framing on every face-move (§6 rule 8) |
| Robotic mechanical arc on a face | A perfectly smooth face-move itself reads as CGI | Intentional handheld (§3.10) on that one beat only |
| Camera teleport / hidden reset between beats | Breaks the one-take illusion the whole concept depends on | Momentum-carry across every seam (§6 rule 10, §8) |

## 8. The flagship continuous take — the full move-by-move path

This is the answer, in one place, to *which of the ten catalog entries the flagship actually
uses, in what order, and where each is deliberately withheld.* Reading the "Catalog entry" column
top to bottom is the proof that the entire **8.0 s primary cut is one unbroken camera path** —
comb → drop → bottle → pour → scalp → hair → face → pack — with no cut and no teleport (Bible
§6, `12-camera-director` R1).

| t (s) | Beat/seam | Catalog entry used | Not used here (and why) |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| 0.0–1.2 | B1 HOOK | **Push-in** (§3.1) + **time-remap** (§3.9, the film's only slow-motion) | No orbit/slider — the beat is a near-locked macro hold, not a travel move |
| 1.2–1.6 | T1 | **Crane-down** (§3.5) + **macro dive** (§3.6) | No whip — the drop-to-strand carry is a physical descent, not a pivot |
| 1.6–3.0 | B2 DISCOVERY | **Crane-up / pull-back** (§3.2/§3.5) + **push-in settle** (§3.1) + **focus rack** (§3.7) | No time-remap — real-time from here to the end, except the withheld B1 slow-mo tail |
| 3.0–3.4 | T2 | **Macro dive** (§3.6) | No crane — this dive is a forward push through the pour, not a vertical journey |
| 3.4–4.8 | B3 RITUAL | **Slider/truck** (§3.4) + **follow-focus** (§3.7) | No orbit/push — the camera tracks the gesture's own axis, it does not approach or circle |
| 4.8–5.2 | T3 | **Whip** (§3.8) | The film's only whip; not repeated anywhere else |
| 5.2–6.6 | B4 TRANSFORMATION | **Push-in** (§3.1) + **intentional handheld** (§3.10, the film's only operated/handheld feel) + **focus rack** (§3.7) | **No** time-remap — the keeper is emphatically real-time so the blink and breath read human |
| 6.6–7.0 | T4 | S-curve deceleration only (§3.9); no dedicated camera-travel entry — the seam is carried by the light-bloom, not by camera motion | No crane/orbit/whip — this transition is a light hand-off, and the camera simply settles |
| 7.0–8.0 | B5 OFFER+CTA | **Orbit** (§3.3) + **focus hold** (§3.7) | No push/pull — the hero pack is presented in the round, not approached or withdrawn from |

**What this proves:** across the whole 8 s, the studio uses **seven of the ten catalog entries**
as actual camera travel (push-in, pull-back, orbit, slider/truck, crane, macro dive, whip), **one**
as a focus discipline layered throughout (focus pull), **one** as a timing discipline applied
everywhere (speed ramp — with its time-remap half spent exactly once), and **one** as a single,
narrowly-scoped exception (intentional handheld, exactly one beat). Nothing is duplicated where it
shouldn't be — there is exactly one slow-motion, exactly one whip, and exactly one handheld feel
in the entire film — and every catalog entry used carries its exit velocity into the next beat's
entry velocity, so the seven-move, ten-second reasoning above renders as a single, unbroken
camera path.
