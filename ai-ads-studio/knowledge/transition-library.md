## 2. The doctrine — "motivated + seamless = ONE continuous movement"

This is the studio's single governing law for every transition in every ad this framework will
ever produce. A transition earns its place only by clearing three gates, in this order:

1. **Motivated.** The narrative *causes* the seam — a falling object meets a falling remedy, a
   pour is followed down, a hand sweeps hair, a shine becomes a light. A transition that exists
   only because it "looks cool" is a gimmick, no matter how elegant the mechanic (§6 of the
   Bible: *the oil drop and the hair strand ARE the transition engines* — motivation is not a
   nice-to-have, it is the thesis).
2. **Physically believable.** A real camera and real physics could produce it — a real drop
   ripples a real oil surface, a real macro probe dives a real pour, real hair genuinely wipes a
   lens. Prefer a **match cut** (two different objects sharing one path or shape) over a **true
   morph** (one object visibly deforming into another) — a morph is the fastest route to the
   "temporal warping" / "morphing bottle" tells (Bible §6.6).
3. **Seamless.** Once motivated and believable, the seam must hide itself — no fade, no
   dip-to-black, no wipe-for-its-own-sake, no split-screen. The outgoing subject of one beat
   physically *becomes* the opening subject of the next. Read end to end, the camera never
   teleports — it is one unbroken path.

**When all three gates clear, the result reads as ONE continuous movement** — not because there
were no cuts (there may have been), but because every join was hidden inside a real, motivated,
physically-believable event. This is the craft principle underneath every entry in §4 below.

### 2.1 The seamlessness ladder (best to worst — reach as high as the scene allows)

| Rank | Technique | Why it ranks here | Library families |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (best) | **Continuous physical camera move** (no cut at all) | Nothing to hide — there is no join | Camera-Motion (§4.5) |
| 2 | **Physical match cut on a shared object** | Two different objects share one screen path; the eye reads continuity because the *motion* never stops | Liquid & Oil (§4.1), Hair (§4.2), Light (§4.3) |
| 3 | **Motion-matched invisible cut** | Precisely matched speed/direction/position hides a real cut inside apparent continuity | Camera-Motion & Invisible (§4.5, #39) |
| 4 | **Object-occlusion invisible cut** | A real foreground object briefly fills the lens, hiding a real cut inside a plausible obstruction | Camera-Motion & Invisible (§4.5, #38) |
| 5 | **Graphic match cut on a shared shape** | Two *different* objects' silhouettes rhyme across a hard cut — reads as "edited," not continuous, but elegant when the rhyme is strong | Object & Match-Cut (§4.4, #17, #18) |
| 6 | **Whip riding real in-frame motion** | A fast pivot disguised inside motion already present — believable only if the motion is real | Hair (§4.2, #10), Camera-Motion (§4.5, #21) |
| 7 | **Cross-dissolve / texture-morph glue** | An editing technique, not a filmed event — use only as connective tissue, never as a locked primary boundary | Texture-Morph (§4.6) |
| 8 (avoid on product) | **True object morph** (one object visibly deforms into another) | Invites the "morphing bottle / temporal warping" tell (§6.6) — reserve only for non-product, clearly-stylized beats, and never on the flagship | none selected; documented as a caution in §4.6 |
| never | **Hard cut / fade / dip-to-black / split-screen / gimmick wipe** | The studio's explicit veto list (Bible §6.6; `15-transition-designer` anti-pattern veto list) | not catalogued — these are the things this whole library exists to replace |

### 2.2 The six-axis scoring rubric (used to score every candidate for a given ad)

Every entry below can be scored 0–10 on these six axes when a run generates its 30+ candidate
slate (`15-transition-designer` Output 6.1). This library states each entry's *general* profile;
the per-ad run scores it against *that ad's* specific boundary.

| Axis | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Motivation** | Does the story cause this seam, or does it just look nice? |
| **Believability** | Could a real camera and real physics produce this? |
| **Seamlessness** | Does it hide the join (§2.1 ladder), or does it read as edited? |
| **Retention** | Does it pull the eye forward into the next beat, or does it pause it? |
| **Brand-safety** | Does it keep the §4 product lock and §5 modesty/restraint intact? |
| **Cost** | Inverse of regeneration risk — how likely is a real render to fail or need a retake? |

### 2.3 Universal negatives every transition in this library must avoid (Bible §6.6)

`temporal warping, frame jitter, banding, liquid color shift, floating objects with no
contact shadow, morphing bottle, changing/warped label, altered logo, mismatched cap color,
HDR halos, oversaturation, flicker, CGI look, low-res/plastic hair, split-screen, hard cut,
fade/dip-to-black, gimmick wipe, watermark, subtitles burn-in.` Every card in §4 names the subset
of these it most risks, so `24-negative-prompt-builder` can weight them where the render is most
exposed.

### 2.4 Duration law

A transition is a **seam**, not a scene. In the flagship's 8 s primary cut the four boundary
transitions (T1–T4) each run **≤ 0.4 s**; an object-resolve transition (the orbit-multiply, §4.4
#15) may extend to fill its own beat's runtime (the flagship gives it 1.0 s). If a mechanic in §4
needs more time than that to read cleanly, it is not a transition — hand the moment back to
`08-storyboard-director` as its own beat.

## 4. The catalog

Each entry: **name · mechanic · when elegant · physical-believability notes · paste-ready prompt
phrasing · failure tell to avoid.** Prompt phrasing follows `cinematography-language.md` §9's
discipline — descriptive, sensory prose for a Veo-3-class model, never bare technical jargon or
camera-brand tokens; bracketed `[placeholders]` mark where a specific ad's nouns are substituted
in.

### 4.1 Family A — Liquid & Oil

> **01 · Drop-drop match-morph** ★ FLAGSHIP (serves T1, Bible §6.1)
> - **Mechanic:** A falling object (the hair strand) and a falling remedy-object (a translucent
>   deep-garnet oil drop) share one continuous downward screen position and vector. On contact the
>   drop lands on an oil surface and a concentric ripple (paired with #27) carries the frame
>   forward. Two *different* objects share one path — a match cut, never a morph of one deforming
>   into the other.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever the story needs a "loss becomes cure" or "problem becomes remedy"
>   beat inside one breath — pairing a pain object with a hero-adjacent object along a shared fall.
> - **Physical believability:** A drop striking a liquid surface produces exactly this radial
>   ripple in real macro photography; nothing here requires an impossible deformation.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a single strand falls and, at the moment it would land, a
>   translucent deep-garnet oil drop falls into the same position from above; the two meet and the
>   frame ripples outward in one continuous downward motion, carrying the camera into the oil
>   surface below."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** temporal warping, frame jitter, banding in the ripple, a liquid
>   color shift, a drop with no visible point of contact or shadow.

> **02 · Splash-bloom wipe**
> - **Mechanic:** The falling drop strikes hard enough to throw a crown of secondary droplets that
>   briefly fill and white out the frame before settling to reveal the next scene.
> - **When elegant:** A punchier, more kinetic pain-to-relief beat on a shorter runtime where the
>   calmer ripple (#01) would feel too slow to earn its transition window.
> - **Physical believability:** Real, but a hard splash reads energetic and slightly chaotic —
>   correct for an athletic/sport-beauty register, wrong for a restrained luxury register.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the drop strikes the oil surface and throws a fine crown of
>   secondary droplets that briefly fill the frame with warm garnet light before settling into the
>   still surface below."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** banding in the droplet crown, an unmotivated white flash, droplets
>   that hang in the air with no gravity.

> **03 · Meniscus-rise**
> - **Mechanic:** The oil's own meniscus (the curved edge where liquid meets a container wall or
>   surface) rises smoothly to swallow the frame from the bottom up — the camera lowers toward the
>   edge, which reads as the surface "rising."
> - **When elegant:** A slower, more contemplative immersion transition where the story wants to
>   feel absorbed into the product rather than struck by an event.
> - **Physical believability:** A camera lowering toward a liquid's edge as the edge appears to
>   rise is a real macro effect — must always be phrased as camera motion, never as liquid
>   defying gravity on its own.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera lowers gently until the oil's curved edge fills
>   the frame, the warm garnet surface rising to swallow the shot completely."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** liquid that appears to rise with no camera motion explaining it;
>   banding across the rising surface.

> **04 · Pour macro-dive** ★ FLAGSHIP (serves T2, Bible §6.1)
> - **Mechanic:** As a ribbon of liquid pours in one unbroken stream, the camera physically pushes
>   forward along the pour's own axis (paired with #20) until the liquid fills the frame, then
>   emerges on the far side into the next scene.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever a controlled pour is already the beat's action and the story needs
>   to travel from "the pour" to "where it lands" without a cut.
> - **Physical believability:** A macro probe lens can genuinely dive along a falling ribbon's own
>   axis; a single clean laminar stream (no turbulence) is what keeps the push readable as real,
>   not compositing.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "as the ribbon of oil pours in a single unbroken stream, the
>   camera pushes forward along its length until translucent garnet and gold light fill the frame
>   completely, then opens on the surface it lands on."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** liquid color shift, banding in the garnet fill, HDR halos on the
>   gold highlight, any warp of a nearby product edge as it leaves frame.

> **05 · Bead-roll**
> - **Mechanic:** A single bead of liquid rolls across a surface (skin, a leaf, a stone plane) and,
>   as it rolls off the lower frame edge, the next scene is already waiting underneath.
> - **When elegant:** A light, unhurried extra sensory beat on a surface distant from the product's
>   own printed identity, where a slow decorative roll won't compete with legibility.
> - **Physical believability:** A bead genuinely rolls under real surface tension and gravity on a
>   tilted plane — fully reproducible.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a single bead of oil rolls slowly across the surface,
>   catching one clean highlight as it moves, and rolls out of frame at the lower edge to reveal
>   the next shot beneath."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** rolling directly across a printed label (risks reading as artwork
>   warp); an unrealistically fast or perfectly straight roll.

> **06 · Immersion-cut**
> - **Mechanic:** The camera follows an object fully beneath a liquid's surface and emerges on the
>   other side into a new scene, treating the liquid as a portal the lens physically swims through.
> - **When elegant:** A slower, more meditative "going under to come back changed" beat — better
>   suited to a spa/ritual register with more runtime than the flagship's 8 s allows.
> - **Physical believability:** A macro probe lens submerging is physically real; light must keep
>   refracting correctly through the liquid the whole way through, or it reads as a digital wipe.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera follows the object down beneath the oil's surface,
>   warm garnet light bending softly around it, and rises back up through the surface into the
>   next scene."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a hard cut disguised as a submersion (no visible refraction); liquid
>   that reads as flat color rather than a real optical medium.

> **27 · Ripple-clock wipe** (the mechanic under #01)
> - **Mechanic:** A concentric ripple, triggered by a contact point (a drop landing, a fingertip
>   touching a surface), expands radially outward at an even rate until it has crossed the frame,
>   revealing the new scene inside its leading edge.
> - **When elegant:** Always paired with a contact-triggered liquid transition (#01) — the physical
>   engine underneath that transition's carry, not a standalone device.
> - **Physical believability:** A real ripple expands at a measurable, even rate, genuinely
>   revealing what lies beneath it as it passes — completely reproducible optics.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a ripple spreads outward from the point of contact in one
>   even wave, and the new scene resolves inside its leading edge as it crosses the frame."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** an uneven or stuttering ripple front; banding in the ripple's
>   highlight; a ripple that reverses direction.

> **31 · Gilded-swirl vignette**
> - **Mechanic:** The liquid's surface swirls inward toward center in a slow spiral, gold
>   highlights trailing the motion, closing the frame into a soft vignette before opening on the
>   next scene.
> - **When elegant:** A decorative closer for a scene with no strong contact-triggered event to
>   motivate a ripple (#27) — reserve for scenes with spare runtime to justify a purely atmospheric
>   beat.
> - **Physical believability:** A real liquid can be made to swirl (stirred, or poured off-axis),
>   but a swirl with no visible cause reads as a graphic effect, not filmed footage.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the oil's surface swirls slowly inward toward the center of
>   frame, gold light trailing the motion until it closes softly into the next scene."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a swirl with no visible motivating cause; oversaturated gold reading
>   as an added graphic. This is the weakest entry in the family on Motivation — reserve for an
>   atmospheric beat, never a locked story boundary.

### 4.2 Family B — Hair

> **07 · Hair-wipe / sweep** ★ FLAGSHIP (serves T3, Bible §6.1)
> - **Mechanic:** A section of hair is swept by hand directly across the lens; the moving strands
>   fill the frame with natural motion-blur, and as they clear, the next scene is already open
>   behind them.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever a hand or head gesture is already moving hair across the frame's
>   foreground — the most versatile "hide a cut inside a real gesture" tool in the whole library.
> - **Physical believability:** Hair swept close to a lens genuinely blurs and wipes it under real
>   motion — a fully in-camera transition, no compositing required.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a section of hair sweeps across the lens, the moving strands
>   filling the frame with natural motion blur, and clears to reveal the next moment as the motion
>   settles."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** low-res or plastic-looking hair in the blur; a hard cut hidden
>   inside the blur instead of a true continuous sweep; a state change (e.g. thin→restored)
>   revealed mid-wipe rather than only after it clears.

> **09 · Braid-unravel morph**
> - **Mechanic:** A styled braid or twist unwinds strand by strand, the unraveling motion itself
>   carrying the frame into the next scene.
> - **When elegant:** A styled, editorial hair story where the unraveling is itself a narrative
>   beat (heritage/ritual unwinding into modern styling) — not for a loose, natural-hair register.
> - **Physical believability:** A real braid can be filmed unraveling; the risk is treating it as a
>   true morph (strands visibly deforming into a new shape) rather than a real physical unbraiding
>   — always describe the latter.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a braided section of hair slowly unwinds strand by strand,
>   the unraveling motion carrying the camera into the next scene as the last strand falls loose."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** strands that visibly deform or dissolve rather than physically
>   unwinding; an unravel speed too fast to read as real hands or real physics.

> **10 · Whip-pan through hair** ▽ (T3 fallback if #07 won't render clean)
> - **Mechanic:** A fast camera pivot rides the blur of hair already in motion (a head turn, a
>   toss) so the pan itself is disguised inside the hair's own motion-blur.
> - **When elegant:** A higher-energy alternative to the calmer sweep (#07) when the beat needs
>   more kinetic urgency, or as its documented fallback.
> - **Physical believability:** A whip-pan riding real hair motion is standard, fully reproducible
>   in-camera technique.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "as her hair moves with the turn of her head, the camera pans
>   quickly to match its motion, the blur of hair carrying the frame into the next shot."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a whip with no hair or motion to ride (reads as a stylistic glitch);
>   motion-blur that looks smeared rather than directional.

> **28 · Hair-curtain part**
> - **Mechanic:** Two sections of hair, hanging or held together like a drawn curtain, are parted
>   by hand or a turn of the head, revealing the next scene as they separate.
> - **When elegant:** A softer, more deliberate reveal than the sweep (#07) — suited to a
>   slower-paced beat with a full second or more to spend on the reveal.
> - **Physical believability:** Hair genuinely parts under light hand pressure or head rotation —
>   real, unhurried, fully reproducible.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "two sections of her hair part gently like a soft curtain, the
>   next scene emerging in the widening gap between them."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** hair parting with no visible hand or head motion causing it (an
>   unmotivated graphic wipe); strands clipping through each other unnaturally as they part.

### 4.3 Family C — Light

> **11 · Light-bloom recede** ★ FLAGSHIP (serves T4, Bible §6.1)
> - **Mechanic:** A specular highlight already visible on a moving surface (hair, skin, liquid)
>   blooms outward into a soft flare filling the frame, then recedes/contracts onto the next
>   scene's own key light — the shine becomes the light source of what follows.
> - **When elegant:** The studio's signature "beauty becomes the light" transition — reserved for
>   the single emotional high point of a cut, where the payoff deserves to illuminate what follows.
> - **Physical believability:** A real specular highlight blooming as a light angle shifts is
>   optically real; keep the bloom a soft, warm, controlled lift — never a blown-out streak — to
>   avoid reading as a digital lens-flare asset.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a warm highlight travels along the moving surface and blooms
>   into a soft flare that fills the frame, then gently recedes to become the light falling on the
>   scene that follows."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** HDR halos, oversaturation, flicker, banding in the bloom, a cool or
>   white flare that breaks a warm color continuity.

> **12 · Lens-flare sweep**
> - **Mechanic:** An anamorphic-style horizontal streak of light sweeps across the frame from one
>   side to the other, briefly whiting out the center before the next scene resolves.
> - **When elegant:** A stylized, overtly "cinematic" transition for a register that welcomes a
>   visible optical flourish — not suited to a restrained, editorial luxury tone.
> - **Physical believability:** Real anamorphic lenses do produce horizontal streak flares off hard
>   specular sources, but a flare with no motivating hard light source in-frame reads as an added
>   digital asset.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a horizontal streak of warm light sweeps across the frame
>   from one edge to the other, briefly filling the center with light before the next scene
>   resolves underneath it."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** HDR halos; a CGI-flare look with no traceable light source;
>   oversaturated color in the streak.

> **13 · Shadow-pass**
> - **Mechanic:** A soft shadow (from an unseen object passing near the light source) sweeps across
>   the frame, darkening it briefly before lifting to reveal the next scene already lit and
>   waiting.
> - **When elegant:** A rare, cooling beat — useful only where the story needs one deliberate
>   moment of dimming before a reveal (a door, a hand, a branch passing near a window).
> - **Physical believability:** A real shadow passing across a lit scene is ordinary optics; the
>   risk here is purely tonal, not physical.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a soft shadow sweeps across the frame as if something passed
>   near the light, briefly dimming the shot before it lifts to reveal the next scene already lit
>   and waiting."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a shadow with no plausible source; dimming a frame that is meant to
>   be building warmth — this transition cools the frame, use with care against a warmth-holding
>   story.

> **14 · Prism-glint**
> - **Mechanic:** A small, sharp glint of refracted light off a curved reflective surface (the
>   liquid's own sheen, a bottle's shoulder) expands outward into a soft rainbow-edged bloom that
>   clears into the next scene.
> - **When elegant:** A jewelry/gem-register moment — well suited to a beat that treats the
>   product's own material as a literal gem for one instant.
> - **Physical believability:** Real prismatic glints occur on curved glass/liquid edges under
>   strong directional light; keep the rainbow fringing subtle or it reads as a lens-artifact
>   preset rather than real dispersion.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a small, sharp glint of light off the curved glass expands
>   into a soft bloom with the faintest edge of rainbow color, clearing gently into the next
>   scene."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** an oversaturated rainbow fringe reading as a stock lens-flare
>   preset; a glint with no visible curved reflective surface causing it.

> **29 · Golden-hour warm-lift** (the exposure logic under #11)
> - **Mechanic:** As a transition beat plays, overall exposure lifts warmly — highlights growing a
>   little brighter and warmer — building toward the bloom (#11) rather than the bloom appearing
>   instantaneously.
> - **When elegant:** Always paired with a bloom-type transition (#11 or #14) as its underlying
>   exposure curve, not used standalone.
> - **Physical believability:** A real warm-lift in exposure across a beat mirrors a camera's own
>   auto-exposure responding to a brightening practical light source — believable as long as it
>   stays gentle.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the light warms and lifts gently through the shot, highlights
>   growing softly brighter, building toward the bloom that follows."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a warm-lift strong enough to clip into overexposure, or a cool/white
>   lift that contradicts the warm continuity the whole spine depends on.

### 4.4 Family D — Object & Match-Cut

> **15 · Orbit-multiply** ★ FLAGSHIP (serves B5 offer resolve, Bible §6.1)
> - **Mechanic:** The camera arcs slowly around a single static hero object; mid-arc, additional
>   identical objects rise smoothly into frame to complete a set (paired with #22, #30), and the
>   orbit eases to a stop on the assembled group.
> - **When elegant:** The studio's signature offer-resolve device — whenever a single-unit hero
>   needs to become a multi-unit pack, or any "the whole set assembles in front of you" reveal,
>   without cutting to a separate shelf shot.
> - **Physical believability:** A slow, motion-control orbit combined with objects rising on cue
>   (via unseen rigging/practical effects) is a standard practical product-shoot technique —
>   nothing here requires anything beyond real camera and real rigging.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera slowly orbits the single bottle; mid-arc, two more
>   identical bottles rise smoothly into place beside it, the camera easing to a stop as the set
>   settles into a clean, resolved frame with a soft natural shadow beneath each."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** any newly-arrived object differing from the original in color,
>   proportions, or markings; a fast or dizzying orbit; objects appearing with no rise motion (a
>   hard cut disguised as a multiply) or casting no shadow.

> **16 · Rack-of-bottles push-in**
> - **Mechanic:** The camera dollies forward to reveal a full set already arranged together, rather
>   than assembling it live.
> - **When elegant:** A faster, lower-drama alternative to the orbit-multiply (#15) for a runtime
>   too tight to afford the assembly beat — trades a small loss of "reveal" magic for speed.
> - **Physical believability:** A straightforward dolly push toward a pre-arranged set — fully
>   real, no rigging required.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera pushes forward to reveal the full set already
>   arranged together, each piece catching one clean highlight as the frame settles."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** framing that makes the set look "shelved" rather than presented —
>   arrange with intention, not a flat row.

> **17 · Comb-to-bottle graphic match**
> - **Mechanic:** A holding object's silhouette or negative-space shape (e.g. a comb's curved
>   spine) is matched to the outline of the next scene's hero object (a bottle's shoulder curve),
>   so the cut lands on a shared shape rather than a shared physical object.
> - **When elegant:** A genuine graphic-match opportunity between two objects whose silhouettes
>   truly rhyme — most useful early in a concepting pass, before a physical-carry transition
>   (§4.1–4.3) has been found for the same boundary.
> - **Physical believability:** A graphic match is a real edit technique (matching outline across a
>   cut) but it is a *cut*, not a continuous physical carry — a lower rung on the §2.1 seamlessness
>   ladder than a match-cut on a shared physical object.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the curved silhouette of [object A] aligns exactly with the
>   curved silhouette of [object B] as the frame cuts between them, the shared shape carrying the
>   eye across the cut."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a genuine shape-morph (one silhouette visibly deforming into the
>   other) — that crosses from a graphic match into a true morph and risks a "morphing" tell on any
>   product-adjacent object; keep it a clean cut on matched shapes, never a deforming animation.

> **18 · Hibiscus-bloom match**
> - **Mechanic:** A flower already in the environment (in the flagship's hero set, the single red
>   hibiscus, §4) opens or turns in a way that graphically matches the framing of the next scene's
>   reveal.
> - **When elegant:** A garnish transition for a beat with a botanical element already in frame and
>   a spare half-second to let it move — never load-bearing on its own for a locked story boundary.
> - **Physical believability:** A flower opening or turning under a light breeze/practical rig move
>   is real and slow enough to film cleanly.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the single red hibiscus turns gently in the light as the
>   frame settles into the next scene behind it."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** using this as the sole carrier of a major story boundary — it is a
>   texture garnish, not a primary engine (see #01/#04/#07/#11/#15 for boundary-carrying weight).

> **30 · Pack-shadow settle** (closes #15)
> - **Mechanic:** As the final hero object(s) come to rest, a real, soft contact shadow forms and
>   settles beneath them, anchoring the closing frame in real light before a cut to end-card or a
>   hold.
> - **When elegant:** Always the closing half-second of an object-resolve transition (paired with
>   #15 or #16) — never a standalone transition.
> - **Physical believability:** Real objects on a real surface under real directional light always
>   cast a soft, correctly-shaped contact shadow; its absence is one of the fastest "floating CGI
>   object" tells.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "as the bottles settle into their final positions, a soft,
>   natural shadow forms beneath each one, anchoring them believably on the surface as the frame
>   holds."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** floating objects with no contact shadow; a shadow that doesn't match
>   the scene's established light direction.

### 4.5 Family E — Camera-Motion & Invisible Cuts

> **19 · Continuous crane down-and-up** ▲ (underlies #01's descent into B2's reveal, T1)
> - **Mechanic:** A single boom/jib move follows a falling object down through a scene, and —
>   without cutting — carries the same momentum back up and out into the next reveal.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever a story needs to physically descend with something falling and then
>   rise with something being discovered, inside one breath — the vertical backbone of any "one
>   continuous take" claim.
> - **Physical believability:** A motion-control jib genuinely executes a smooth down-then-up arc;
>   the downward velocity at the bottom must match the upward velocity beginning the rise — no
>   reset, no hidden cut disguised as continuity.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera follows the falling object straight down, and
>   without cutting, rises back up and out to reveal what comes next, the motion carried through
>   with no break in momentum."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a visible height "reset" between the down and the up — the descent
>   and the rise must share one unbroken momentum curve.

> **20 · Push-through** ▲ (the mechanic under #04, T2)
> - **Mechanic:** The camera physically dollies forward through a translucent or semi-transparent
>   medium already filling the frame (liquid, a sheer drape, fog) until it emerges on the far side.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever a pour, a drape, or a comparable translucent medium already exists
>   in the world of the shot and the story needs to travel through it rather than cut around it.
> - **Physical believability:** A macro probe or standard dolly pushing through a real translucent
>   medium is completely real, provided the medium's own material properties (liquid clarity,
>   fabric weave) stay consistent through the push.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera pushes forward through the translucent medium
>   filling the frame until it emerges cleanly on the other side into the next scene."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a "CGI fly-through" look where the medium doesn't behave like a real
>   substance; banding or color shift partway through the push.

> **21 · Whip-pan hard**
> - **Mechanic:** A fast, aggressive camera pivot with heavy motion blur, used purely as an
>   energetic scene-to-scene device rather than riding an existing in-frame motion.
> - **When elegant:** A higher-energy, more "native platform" register where aggressive pacing is
>   correct — not suited to a restrained luxury tone (reserve #10's hair-ridden version for that
>   register instead).
> - **Physical believability:** A genuine, real-camera technique; the risk is purely tonal — a hard
>   whip with nothing motivating it inside the frame reads as an edited stylistic insert rather
>   than a filmed moment.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera whip-pans hard across the frame in a fast blur,
>   settling cleanly on the next scene as the motion resolves."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** using this in a restrained luxury register where it reads as "too
>   aggressive" against the tone; a whip with zero in-frame motivation.

> **22 · Arc-around** ▲ (the mechanic under #15, B5)
> - **Mechanic:** A slow, even angular camera move around a static subject, executed on a
>   motion-control or slider-arc rig.
> - **When elegant:** Always the underlying camera technique for any product "in the round" reveal
>   (paired with #15's multiply event, or used alone for a single-hero orbit with no multiply).
> - **Physical believability:** A completely standard, fully reproducible practical-product-shoot
>   rig move.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera arcs slowly and evenly around the subject, holding
>   it centered and sharp throughout the turn."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** an uneven or accelerating arc; letting the hero subject soften or
>   leave frame center during the turn.

> **23 · Speed-ramp glide** ▲ (underlies the HOOK-to-T1 slow-motion tail)
> - **Mechanic:** A move's own travel velocity eases into and out of a brief slow-motion window,
>   decelerating smoothly into the ramp and accelerating back out of it, rather than snapping
>   between speeds.
> - **When elegant:** Underlying any move that needs to carry a beat from real-time into (or out
>   of) the studio's single rationed slow-motion moment without a visible speed "jump."
> - **Physical believability:** A real camera rig or a real overcranked capture ramping smoothly to
>   and from a target frame rate is standard technique; an instant, snapping speed change is the
>   tell that betrays a digital time-remap rather than a filmed ramp.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the motion eases smoothly into a gentle slow-motion glide and
>   eases back out to real time, with no sudden jump in speed at either end."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a hard, snapping speed change; more than one ramped moment in the
>   same cut — slow-motion is rationed to a single beat per the studio's pacing law.

> **32 · Match-on-action turn** ▲ (hands #07's sweep into the TRANSFORMATION turn, T3)
> - **Mechanic:** A gesture already in motion at the end of one beat (a hand sweeping hair, a head
>   starting to turn) is the exact motion that opens the next beat, so any cut is hidden entirely
>   inside a continuous human movement.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever a beat naturally ends mid-gesture and the next beat's opening action
>   is a direct physical continuation of that same gesture — the most human, least "edited"
>   transition in the library.
> - **Physical believability:** This is simply how continuity editing already works in real
>   filmmaking (a cut on movement is imperceptible to the eye) — completely reproducible, and when
>   done as one uncut continuous take requires no cut at all.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "as the gesture completes, it carries directly into the next
>   movement, the same motion continuing without interruption into the turn that follows."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a visible pause or reset between the "end" of the first gesture and
>   the "start" of the second — the two motions must read as one.

> **38 · Object-pass-through invisible cut**
> - **Mechanic:** The camera moves close enough to a foreground object (a drape's fold, an arch's
>   pillar, a hand passing near the lens) that it briefly and naturally fills/darkens the frame; a
>   real cut is hidden inside that brief occlusion.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever the set already contains a foreground element the camera can
>   credibly pass close to — the studio's cleanest "hide a hard cut" tool when no other engine
>   (liquid, hair, light) is available at a given boundary.
> - **Physical believability:** A camera passing close enough to a real foreground object to
>   briefly occlude the lens is ordinary handheld/dolly technique; the occlusion must be brief (a
>   few frames) or it reads as an accidental obstruction rather than a deliberate device.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera moves past the fold of the drape close enough that
>   it briefly fills the frame, and as it clears, the next scene is already there."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** an occlusion long enough to read as a genuine interruption or as
>   clumsily-obstructed footage; using this where no foreground object plausibly exists to pass.

> **39 · Motion-matched invisible cut**
> - **Mechanic:** Two different shots are cut together at the exact frame where their subjects'
>   on-screen motion — speed, direction, and screen position — matches, so the eye reads the join
>   as one continuous movement rather than a cut.
> - **When elegant:** The most technically demanding but most powerful "hidden cut" in the library
>   — reserved for a boundary where a true physical-carry engine (§4.1–4.3) doesn't exist, but two
>   shots' motion can still be precisely matched.
> - **Physical believability:** A standard high-end editorial technique (match-on-motion); it
>   requires no impossible physics at all, only precise timing.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the motion continues at the exact same speed and direction
>   across the cut, so the movement reads as one unbroken action rather than two separate shots."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** any mismatch in speed, direction, or screen position at the join —
>   even a small one reads instantly as a hard cut.

### 4.6 Family F — Texture-Morph

> These three entries are the library's **cross-dissolve family** — an editing technique, not a
> filmed physical event. They sit deliberately low on the §2.1 seamlessness ladder (rank 7 of 8)
> and should be used only as **connective glue between two other engines**, never as a locked
> primary boundary transition. This is also the family where the doctrine's morph caution (§2,
> gate 2) is most relevant: none of these dissolve one *object's identity* into another — they
> rhyme two *textures* — and none may ever be applied to the product itself (Bible §4, §6.6
> "morphing bottle" is an explicit veto).

> **24 · Oil-sheen to hair-shine dissolve**
> - **Mechanic:** The specular texture of one surface (liquid's glossy sheen) is visually rhymed
>   with the specular texture of the next surface (hair's shine-ribbon) via a soft cross-dissolve
>   between the two textures, rather than a hard cut.
> - **When elegant:** A connective-tissue beat between a product/liquid moment and a hair moment,
>   when neither a physical-carry engine (§4.1–4.3) nor a match-cut (§4.4 #17/#32) is available at
>   that exact boundary.
> - **Physical believability:** A cross-dissolve is an editing technique, not a filmed physical
>   event — it reads as "edited," a lower rung than a true continuous move; reserve it for
>   connective glue only.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the glossy sheen on the oil's surface softly dissolves into
>   the same warm shine now moving along the hair, the two textures rhyming as one carries into the
>   other."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** visible cross-fade "ghosting" of two unrelated images — the texture
>   rhyme must be strong enough that the dissolve reads as a match, not a generic fade.

> **25 · Steam/warmth haze**
> - **Mechanic:** A soft warm haze or heat-shimmer briefly diffuses the frame, bridging two beats
>   through an atmospheric wash rather than a hard join.
> - **When elegant:** Rarely, and only in a genuinely warm/steam-adjacent physical context (a bath,
>   a warm towel) — the flagship's register has no such context, so this entry is documented for
>   future products, not for the flagship.
> - **Physical believability:** Real, but a haze with no visible heat source reads exactly like a
>   stock "dreamy fade" transition preset — one of the studio's explicitly vetoed clichés.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "a soft warmth briefly hazes the frame, the next scene
>   resolving gently as it clears."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** this reads as a fade in disguise (Bible §6.6 "fade" applies); do not
>   use unless a real, visible heat source justifies the haze in-world.

> **26 · Grain-to-grain match**
> - **Mechanic:** The out-of-focus background texture (bokeh field, grain character) of the
>   outgoing shot is matched to the incoming shot's own background texture, so a hard cut
>   underneath two matched textures reads calmer and more continuous than it otherwise would.
> - **When elegant:** A subtle continuity aid layered underneath any other transition (never a
>   standalone device) — it lowers the visual "jump" of a cut without doing any of the transition's
>   actual work.
> - **Physical believability:** Matching grain/bokeh character across two real lens setups on the
>   same camera body is standard high-end post practice; it isn't a "transition" so much as
>   continuity insurance.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing (apply as a continuity note, not a standalone clause):** "the
>   out-of-focus texture behind the subject carries the same soft, warm character into the next
>   shot."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** two visibly different bokeh characters (one clinical/sharp, one
>   soft/warm) sitting on either side of a cut — reads as two different cameras/looks.

### 4.7 Family G — Reflection

> **33 · Mirror-pass (reflection swap)**
> - **Mechanic:** The camera pushes into a reflection visible in a real reflective surface in-frame
>   (glass, a polished stone plinth, a liquid's own surface) until the reflection itself fills the
>   frame and becomes the next scene's live action, rather than a reflected image.
> - **When elegant:** Whenever the set already contains a genuinely reflective surface (the hero
>   set's stone/travertine podium, the bottle's own glass, §4) that can credibly hold a second
>   image within the first.
> - **Physical believability:** A real reflection pushed into by a macro lens genuinely reveals
>   more of what it reflects as the lens nears it — fully reproducible, provided the reflected
>   geometry (perspective, warp) stays correct throughout the push.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera pushes into the reflection on the polished stone
>   surface until it fills the frame, and what was a reflection becomes the scene itself."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a reflection with visibly wrong perspective/geometry (an easy tell
>   that it's a graphic overlay, not a real optical reflection); a reflective surface with no
>   plausible material reason to be reflective.

> **34 · Catchlight-swap**
> - **Mechanic:** A small specular catchlight already visible in a reflective element (an eye, a
>   bottle's shoulder, a bead of oil) grows and becomes the light source that illuminates the next
>   scene — a quieter cousin of the light-bloom (#11), sourced from a reflection rather than a
>   direct highlight.
> - **When elegant:** A smaller-scale alternative to the full light-bloom for a lower-stakes
>   boundary — a connective beat rather than a film's single emotional peak.
> - **Physical believability:** A catchlight genuinely can brighten as a light source moves closer
>   or a reflective surface turns toward it; keep the growth gradual and warm to stay believable.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the small point of light caught in the reflection grows
>   warmer and brighter, gently becoming the light that falls across the next scene."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** an instant catchlight "pop" with no gradual growth; a cool-toned
>   catchlight that breaks the warm continuity.

> **35 · Water/oil-surface flip**
> - **Mechanic:** An inverted reflection sitting on a still liquid surface (the upside-down mirror
>   image of what's above it) rights itself into the true, upright image as the camera rises past
>   the surface line.
> - **When elegant:** A more overtly "trick" reveal for a beat that wants a small moment of visual
>   surprise (what looked like the real scene was its own reflection) — spend sparingly, once per
>   film at most.
> - **Physical believability:** A still liquid surface genuinely holds a correct inverted
>   reflection, and a camera crossing that surface line genuinely transitions from reflection to
>   reality — real, but the flip must be timed to the exact moment the lens crosses the surface
>   plane or it reads as a digital flip effect.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the inverted reflection on the still surface rights itself
>   into the true image as the camera rises past the waterline, revealing the real scene above."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a flip that happens before or after the camera actually crosses the
>   surface plane (breaks the physical logic); a reflection whose geometry doesn't match what it's
>   supposedly reflecting.

### 4.8 Family H — Scale (Macro ↔ Wide)

> **36 · Macro-to-wide pull-reveal**
> - **Mechanic:** The shot opens on an extreme macro detail and physically pulls back (dolly-out or
>   crane-rise) far enough that the detail resolves as one small part of a much larger scene.
> - **When elegant:** A "the intimate becomes the whole picture" reveal — well suited to opening a
>   film on a single detail (a droplet, a strand) before disclosing the full product/set it belongs
>   to.
> - **Physical believability:** A continuous physical pull-back genuinely and gradually reduces
>   magnification — completely real, provided the pull stays smooth and doesn't snap through
>   intermediate scales.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the extreme macro detail slowly resolves into the wider scene
>   as the camera pulls back, revealing what it was always part of."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** a pull that jumps in scale rather than continuously reducing
>   magnification (reads as a cross-dissolve, not one continuous move); a reveal that discloses
>   nothing new (unmotivated pull — see `camera-movements.md` §3.2).

> **37 · Wide-to-macro dive-in**
> - **Mechanic:** The reverse of #36 — the shot opens wide and the camera physically pushes/dives
>   in until a single detail fills the frame at macro scale, becoming the opening of the next beat.
> - **When elegant:** A scene-setting opener that wants to end on an intimate, specific detail
>   rather than staying wide — establishes context first, then earns the intimacy.
> - **Physical believability:** A continuous physical push-in that increases magnification smoothly
>   to macro scale is real, provided focus tracks the target detail continuously through the dive
>   (no focus hunting).
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the camera pushes steadily forward from the wide scene until
>   a single detail fills the frame at macro scale, focus following it continuously all the way
>   in."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** focus hunting or snapping to the macro target rather than tracking
>   continuously; a dive so fast it reads as an optical zoom rather than a physical push (never
>   optical, per the studio's camera doctrine — `camera-movements.md` §6 rule 1).

### 4.9 Family I — Focus-Pull

> **08 · Strand-focus rack**
> - **Mechanic:** The lens racks focus from one strand/plane in the foreground to a different
>   strand/plane behind it, using the shift in what's sharp — not a camera or subject move — to
>   carry the frame into a new emphasis.
> - **When elegant:** A subtler, quieter transition option than a physical sweep (#07) for a beat
>   that wants to change emphasis without any object crossing the lens.
> - **Physical believability:** A directed follow-focus rack between two named planes is completely
>   standard cinematography, provided it never hunts or breathes (see failure tell).
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "focus shifts smoothly from the strand in the foreground to
>   the one behind it, the new plane resolving crisply as the first one softens."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** focus hunting (visibly searching before landing) or focus breathing
>   on the hold afterward — both are fast tells of a generated or poorly operated shot.

> **40 · Defocus-to-refocus wipe**
> - **Mechanic:** The lens racks entirely out of focus until the frame is a soft, abstract wash of
>   color and light, then refocuses on an entirely different scene — total defocus (rather than a
>   partial rack) is the bridge.
> - **When elegant:** The gentlest, most "invisible" focus-based transition — useful when no plane
>   exists to rack between (#08 requires two named planes; this needs only one starting plane and a
>   soft landing).
> - **Physical believability:** A full rack to maximum defocus and back is a real lens operation;
>   because the defocused wash carries almost no information, always initiate it from a plane that
>   was already sharp, never open on a blur, or it reads as an unmotivated fade rather than a focus
>   event.
> - **Paste-ready prompt phrasing:** "the lens softens out of focus until the frame dissolves into
>   warm, abstract color, then sharpens again on the next scene."
> - **Failure tell to avoid:** opening the transition already out of focus (no visible "before"
>   sharp plane, reading as an unmotivated fade); a defocus so brief it registers as a glitch
>   rather than a deliberate rack.

## 6. Anti-pattern checklist — what every selected transition must clear

Before any entry from §4 is handed to `08-storyboard-director` or compiled by
`26-kie-prompt-builder`, confirm:

- [ ] **Motivated** — the story causes this seam; it is not present "because it looks cool" (§2,
      gate 1).
- [ ] **Physically believable** — a real camera + real physics could produce it; a match cut is
      used wherever a true morph would otherwise be needed (§2, gate 2).
- [ ] **Duration** — a boundary transition (T-type) runs **≤ 0.4 s**; an object-resolve transition
      (like #15) stays inside its own beat's runtime (§2.4).
- [ ] **Product lock intact** — no engine morphs, warps, recolors, or relabels the bottle/cap/
      liquid/label (Bible §4); the three-bottle multiply in #15 uses **identical** locks.
- [ ] **Warm continuity** — color temperature holds warm end-to-end across the seam; no cool
      flare, no dip-to-black, no HDR halo (Bible §6.6).
- [ ] **No banned mechanic used** — no hard cut, no fade/dip-to-black, no split-screen, no
      gimmick/glitch/whoosh-swipe preset (Bible §6.6; §2.1 ladder, "never" row).
- [ ] **No unmotivated float** — every camera-motion entry (§4.5) names its motivation and its
      stopping governor; a move without one is drift, not direction.
- [ ] **Fewer than 30 scored candidates presented** — a per-ad selection is incomplete without the
      full doctrine slate (`15-transition-designer` R7); this library's 40 entries are the shelf
      that slate is drawn from.
- [ ] **Prompt phrasing is descriptive, not technical** — paste-ready phrasing above translates
      every mechanic into sensory prose for `kie_veo3`; no bare camera-department jargon or brand
      tokens land in the compiled positive paragraph (`cinematography-language.md` §9).
