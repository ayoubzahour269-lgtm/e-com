## 2. Buyer psychology of hair loss & thinning

Hair-loss/thinning is one of the most emotionally charged categories in beauty marketing because
the product sits at the intersection of **appearance, identity, and a history of being let down**.
The Bible's audience profile (§3) — women ~22–45, primarily Saudi, "concerned with shedding,
thinning, visible scalp gaps; have tried many oils without result" — describes a buyer who is
simultaneously **hurting** and **skeptical**. Both must be answered, in that order, or the ad
fails: comfort without proof reads as empty; proof without comfort reads cold.

**Why hair loss triggers identity threat, not just cosmetic concern.** Hair is one of the most
visible, controllable markers of femininity and vitality in the cultures this studio serves
(Bible §5). Its thinning is rarely experienced as a neutral cosmetic fact — it is read by the
woman herself as a signal about aging, health, or attractiveness slipping out of her control.
This is why the Bible's dominant emotional trigger is **hope + restored femininity**, not merely
"a solution that works" (Bible §6, emotional spine: Pain → Heritage/Trust → Ritual/Self-care →
Transformation → Pride → Action). The creative must metabolize a felt loss into felt agency —
"there is something I can *do*" — before it can sell a bottle.

**The awareness ladder (a named, industry-standard framework — not a Mechat-specific invention),
mapped onto the Bible's audience and pain points:**

| Awareness stage | Buyer's internal state | Bible evidence this studio has | Beat that meets her here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Unaware** | Doesn't yet register thinning as a "problem," just notices something feels off | Not the flagship's target — she isn't the primary buyer of an 8 s direct-response cut | Not addressed by UNBROKEN THREAD |
| **Problem-aware** | Feels the shedding, sees the strand fall, hasn't looked for a fix yet | Pain points: "hair falling with every comb stroke; gaps appearing" (§3) | HOOK (0.0–1.2 s): the comb macro names her exact felt moment |
| **Solution-aware** | Knows "oils/treatments exist," hasn't found one she trusts | Objection: "wasted money on oils that did nothing" (§3) | DISCOVERY (1.6–3.0 s): heritage trust interrupts generic-solution fatigue |
| **Product-aware** | Knows Mechat exists, is evaluating it specifically | Trust elements: 4.8/5 rating, verified reviews, COD/inspect-before-pay (§3) | OFFER+CTA (7.0–8.0 s): the honest offer and risk-reversal close the evaluation |
| **Most-aware** | Ready to buy, just needs the mechanics (price, delivery, payment) | Offer: 139 SAR/3-pack/free shipping/COD (§3) | CTA line: «اطلبي الآن — الدفع عند الاستلام» (*uṭlubī al-ān — ad-dafʿ ʿind al-istilām*, "Order now — Cash on delivery," Bible §6.3) |

The flagship's 8 s primary cut is built almost entirely for **Problem-aware through Product-aware**
viewers — it does not attempt Unaware-stage education, because an 8 s Meta/TikTok/Snap hook window
(Bible §5) has no room for it.

**The five recurring pain moments (from Bible §3's pain-point list, expanded into the concrete
sensory instants a hair-thinning buyer actually lives through — this is elaboration of Bible
facts, not new claims):**

| Pain moment | Emotional read | What she is telling herself | Where the flagship answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The comb/brush moment | Immediate, visceral, repeats daily | "Again. Every single time." | HOOK macro — the falling strand IS this moment, filmed close and quiet (Bible §6.1) |
| The shower drain / pillow | Quantified evidence, hard to ignore | "This is worse than I thought." | Implied by the hook line's overlay: «كل تمشيطة… وشعرك ينقص؟» (*kull tamshīṭa… wa-shaʿruki yanquṣ?*, "Every comb stroke… and your hair keeps thinning?", Bible §6.3) |
| The part-line / scalp-gap check in the mirror | Private, shame-adjacent, rarely voiced aloud | "Can other people see this?" | Never shown literally (Bible's negatives forbid fear-mongering about the body, §5) — addressed only through the *resolution*, not by dwelling on the gap |
| The graveyard of failed products | Skepticism, sunk-cost fatigue | "Why would this one be different?" | Heritage device — «سرّ جداتنا» (*sirru jaddātinā*, "our grandmothers' secret," Bible §6.3) reframes the category from "another product" to "a trusted, older answer" |
| The hope she is afraid to have again | Guarded optimism | "I want to believe this, but I've been disappointed before." | TRANSFORMATION beat's *real* blink and breath (Bible §6.1) — an earned, human reveal, not a hyped claim, is what lets guarded hope open |

**Ethical guardrail (owned in full by `02-consumer-psychology`, restated here as the floor this
document never crosses):** every trigger this studio uses rests on a fact already true in Bible
§3 — a real pain, a real heritage story, a real standing discount, a real risk-reversal. This
studio manufactures **no** urgency, **no** scarcity, and **no** fear about the body (Bible §5).
Momentum comes from resolved emotion, never a fake countdown or a dramatized scalp close-up.

## 3. Claims discipline & compliance — avoiding medical overreach

Hair-care is a category where **cosmetic claims and medical/drug claims sit close together in
language but far apart in what they promise** — a cosmetic claim describes appearance, sensation,
or a beautifying effect ("softer," "shinier," "less visible shedding"); a medical/drug claim
describes altering the body's structure or function, treating a diagnosed condition, or curing a
disease ("regrows hair for androgenic alopecia," "blocks DHT," "clinically proven regrowth in
90 days"). Ad platforms (Meta, TikTok, Snap) and Gulf ad-standards bodies scrutinize this
category specifically because it has a long history of overreach. **This is craft discipline for
this studio, not a substitute for legal/regulatory review** — but no drafted line should even
approach the medical side of that line, because Bible §3 never makes a medical claim and this
studio never adds one.

**The four Bible-locked claims are the ceiling, not the floor for elaboration.** Every skill that
writes a headline, overlay, VO line, or on-screen claim must be able to point to one of these four
sentences (Bible §3) as its source:

| # | Bible-locked claim (verbatim intent) | What it IS saying | What it is NOT saying |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stops hair loss from the first weeks — strengthens & anchors follicles; less shedding in the comb and on the pillow | A reduction in *shedding*, felt within the FAQ's stated 3–5 week window | Not a cure for baldness, not a claim about diagnosed alopecia, not "permanent" |
| 2 | Stimulates new hair growth — Moroccan herbs activate scalp circulation, waking dormant follicles | A *stimulation* mechanism framed as circulation/activation, not a pharmacological one | Not "regrows all lost hair," not a percentage/statistic, not "clinically proven," not a named active-ingredient mechanism (no DHT, no peptides, no drug terms) |
| 3 | Noticeable density & length — with regular use 2–3×/week | A visible-density outcome **conditioned on** the stated regular-use cadence, within the FAQ's 2–3 month window | Not a guarantee, not a specific percentage or centimeter figure, not "works for everyone" |
| 4 | Shine & softness from first use — coats the strand, silky, non-greasy | An immediate sensory/cosmetic effect | Not a claim about hair-fiber structural repair or "clinical" cosmetic-science language |

**Word substitution table — the compliant Mechat vocabulary vs. the words that cross into medical
overreach (for any skill drafting a headline, overlay, or VO line):**

| Banned / risky (medical, absolute, or unverifiable) | Bible-safe replacement | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Cure(s) baldness / hair loss" | "Stops hair loss from the first weeks" (Claim 1, verbatim) | "Cure" implies a medical resolution of a diagnosed condition; the Bible claims a shedding reduction, not a cure |
| "Clinically proven," "dermatologist-tested/recommended," "FDA-approved" | "100% natural," "authentic Moroccan grandmothers' recipe" (Bible §3 positioning) | No clinical trial, dermatologist endorsement, or regulatory approval exists in Bible §3 — do not imply one |
| "Regrows all your hair," "reverses baldness" | "Stimulates new hair growth" (Claim 2, verbatim) | "Regrows/reverses" implies a guaranteed, total outcome; the Bible claims stimulation, not restoration to a prior state |
| "Guaranteed results," "works for everyone" | "With regular use 2–3×/week" (Claim 3's own conditioning clause) | The Bible conditions its density claim on cadence; drop the conditioning clause and the claim becomes a guarantee it isn't |
| "Blocks DHT," "follicle-stimulating peptides," any named active-compound mechanism | "Moroccan herbs activate scalp circulation" (Claim 2, verbatim) | Bible §3 lists four whole-ingredient categories (§5 below), never an isolated active compound or a pharmacological mechanism |
| "Overnight," "instant regrowth," "in one use" | "From first use" reserved ONLY for shine/softness (Claim 4) — never for growth or density | Claim 4's "first use" language covers sensory effect only; applying it to growth/density claims collapses the FAQ's real 3–5 week / 2–3 month timelines |
| "Treats/cures alopecia, androgenetic hair loss, or any named medical condition" | "Suits all hair types — dry, oily, colored, treated" (Bible §3 FAQ) | Bible never names a diagnosed medical condition; naming one reframes a cosmetic product as a drug/treatment |
| "Scientifically formulated," "lab-developed," "proprietary complex" | "Traditional Moroccan herbal hair oil," "wasfat al-jaddāt" (heritage positioning, §7 below) | The Bible's authority device is heritage, not laboratory science — borrowing pseudo-clinical language undercuts the true positioning and risks an unverifiable claim |

**Timeframe discipline.** The only result timelines this studio may state are the ones in Bible
§3's FAQ: **softness/shine — first use; less shedding — 3–5 weeks; noticeable density — 2–3
months**, always paired with the **2–3×/week regular-use** condition (Claim 3). Never compress
these windows for a punchier line — a faster-sounding claim is an invented claim.

**Before/after and testimonial claims are governed by §4 below**; platform ad-policy note: Meta,
TikTok, and Snap all apply heightened scrutiny to personal-appearance and health-adjacent
creative — expect claim reviews, restrictions on implying "before/after" body-image change, and
limits on targeting by personal health attributes. The single best protection this studio has
against a rejection or a compliance strike is that **every line is already traceable to Bible §3
and never needs to reach further.**

## 4. Ethical before/after

Before/after is the single most abused device in this category — split-screen photo composites,
lighting tricks, extensions swapped in for the "after" shot, and timelines compressed into a
30-second illusion. This studio's structural answer, locked at the concept level (Bible §6), is
also its ethics answer: **the transformation happens inside one continuous, unbroken camera move
— not a cut, not a split screen, not a photo composite.** A viewer cannot be shown a fabricated
result if there is no edit point at which the footage could have been swapped.

**The rules:**

1. **One continuous take, one model, one lighting continuity, start to finish.** The
   TRANSFORMATION beat (Bible §6.1, 5.2–6.6 s) is reached by the *same* unbroken thread that
   opened on the HOOK strand — never a hard cut from a "before" shot to a separately filmed
   "after" shot. This is simultaneously a realism requirement (Bible §6.6 forbids "morphing"
   artifacts) and an honesty requirement: continuity is the proof there was no swap.
2. **No wig, extension, or hairpiece substitution at the reveal.** The hair shown in
   TRANSFORMATION must read as a continuation of the same hair shown at HOOK — denser, longer,
   and more luminous as an *aspirational, symbolic* rendering of the stated benefit, never as a
   literal single-application miracle. (Wig/extension-swap detection is 16-hair-realism-
   director's realism tell; here it is additionally an honesty tell.)
3. **The transformation is illustrative of the stated outcome over the stated timeline, not a
   literal 8-second result.** Nothing in the cut claims the visible change happens in eight
   seconds — the VO and overlays never assert a timeframe faster than the FAQ's (§3 above). The
   beat visually *symbolizes* what regular 2–3×/week use delivers over 2–3 months; it does not
   claim to have filmed that elapsed time.
4. **No photo manipulation, no digital hair-density "sweetening," no color-graded scalp
   coverage tricks.** Any post-production technique that alters hair density, length, or fullness
   beyond ordinary color grading is a fabricated result, full stop — regardless of platform,
   regardless of whether a disclaimer is present.
5. **Testimonial and rating claims stay literal.** The Bible's trust elements — 4.8/5 rating,
   verified-purchase reviews (Riyadh, Jeddah, Dammam), Bible §3 — may be referenced only as the
   real mechanics they are (a rating, a review system); this studio never fabricates a quoted
   review, a named customer, or a specific "I lost X, now I have Y" testimonial claim that isn't
   sourced from an actual review.
6. **No split-screen, no side-by-side grid, no "swipe" before/after UI element.** These formats
   are both a luxury-craft tell (10-luxury-commercial-director calls split-screen a cheap-ad tell,
   borrowed from L'Oréal's mass-market code and deliberately left behind — see
   `knowledge/luxury-beauty-ad-language.md` §1) and a compliance risk (platforms flag literal
   before/after grids for extra review). The single continuous move satisfies both concerns at
   once.

**Do / Don't:**

| DO | DON'T |
|---|---|
| Show transformation as one unbroken camera thread | Cut to a separately shot/composited "after" |
| Let the TRANSFORMATION beat symbolize the stated 2–3-month outcome | Claim the visible result happened within the ad's runtime |
| Keep the same model, same hair (denser/longer/shinier), same lighting world | Swap in a wig, extension, or a different model's hair for the reveal |
| Reference the real 4.8/5 rating and verified-review mechanic | Invent a quoted testimonial or a specific named result |
| Use ordinary color grading (warm, filmic, per §3 palette) | Use density-sweetening, scalp-coverage retouching, or hair-count digital enhancement |
| Let the reveal read as earned (real blink, real breath, Bible §6.1) | Force a reveal via effects, glitter/sparkle overlays, or an exaggerated "reveal whoosh" |

## 5. Ingredient storytelling

Mechat's authority comes from **heritage and whole-ingredient honesty**, not laboratory science
(§3 above) — so ingredient storytelling must render each ingredient as a **sensory, provenance-
rich character**, never as a pseudo-clinical "active compound." The Bible names exactly four
ingredients (§3); this studio never adds a fifth, never isolates a "key active" from within one of
the four, and never assigns a mechanism the Bible doesn't state.

**The four ingredients — function, sensory storytelling angle, and the language boundary:**

| Ingredient (Arabic, translit, gloss) | Bible-stated function | Sensory storytelling angle | Banned pseudo-clinical language |
|---|---|---|---|
| **المشاط / قشور الجوز** — *al-Mishāṭ / qushūr al-jawz* ("Mishāṭ / walnut husk") | The traditional secret to strengthen roots & stop shedding | Root, earth, strength — filmed/spoken as the anchor ingredient the whole product is named for; "traditional secret" language, not a compound name | "Juglone extract," "tannin-active complex," any isolated-compound naming |
| **الكركديه الأحمر** — *al-karkadīh al-aḥmar* ("red hibiscus") | Antioxidants, stimulates growth, warm tone | Color, warmth, vitality — the visual/verbal echo of the garnet liquid itself (Bible §4); a natural bridge between ingredient and product-macro shots | "Anthocyanin-rich," "antioxidant index," any lab-metric framing |
| **الحناء الطبيعية** — *al-ḥinnāʾ aṭ-ṭabīʿiyya* ("natural henna") | Coats & protects the strand, adds density & shine | Coating, protection, the shine-ribbon vocabulary (`knowledge/luxury-beauty-ad-language.md` §4) — a tactile, visible-on-hair ingredient | "Lawsone-based coating agent," "cuticle-sealing polymer" |
| **زيوت نباتية مغذية** — *zuyūt nabātiyya mughadhdhiya* ("nourishing plant oils") | Hydrate scalp, nourish root→tip | Warmth, richness, the root-to-tip gradient vocabulary — oils as the carrier that makes the ritual feel indulgent, not medicinal | "Fatty-acid profile," "lipid barrier repair," any dermatological framing |

**Origin-story discipline.** The Bible's provenance claim is exactly this much: *"a traditional
Moroccan herbal hair oil"* at *"a modern concentration,"* free of parabens and silicone (§3). This
studio may narrate that provenance sensorially — Moroccan herbs, an old recipe, a warm climate,
generations of use — but it **never invents a specific founding year, a named founder, a specific
village, a certification body, or a "passed down since [date]" claim** that Bible §3 does not
state. Heritage storytelling stays evocative and general; it does not fabricate a false specific
to sound more credible — a fabricated specific is itself an invented claim, and specificity that
sounds verifiable but isn't is a worse compliance risk than an honest generality.

**The "three ingredient rule" for skills drafting copy or scene direction:** before any line
names an ingredient, confirm it is one of the four above, confirm the function claimed matches
its Bible-stated function exactly, and confirm no isolated "active" or lab-metric language has
been attached to it. If a draft line fails any of the three checks, it does not ship.

## 6. Ritual framing

Framing product use as a **ritual** rather than a **treatment** does two things at once: it
deepens the emotional payoff (self-care, restored femininity — Bible §6's secondary/primary
triggers) and it keeps language on the cosmetic, not medical, side of §3's line (a "ritual" is
performed for pleasure and care; a "treatment" is administered for a condition). The Bible's own
structure already frames Mechat this way — "a modern concentration" of "grandmothers' recipe,"
applied "2–3×/week" (§3), not a daily clinical regimen with a dosage.

**The RITUAL beat (Bible §6.1, 3.4–4.8 s) is the studio's ritual-framing template — steps, sensory
cue, and emotional payoff:**

| Ritual step | Sensory cue rendered | Emotional payoff | Claims-safe framing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fingertip massage into roots/scalp | Warmth, pressure, practical hand-to-scalp contact (`luxury-beauty-ad-language.md` §4: "practical contact") | A moment of self-administered care — she is doing something *for herself*, not enduring a procedure | "Massaged in," "worked into the roots" — never "applied as directed" or dosage-style language |
| Glide down a section of healthy hair | Light catching strands, the shine-ribbon vocabulary | The care extends from scalp to ends — the whole ritual, not a spot treatment | "Coats the strand" (Claim 4, verbatim) |
| Section catches light, settles | Root-to-tip gradient, natural weight/bounce | A small, private moment of noticing something already feels different | "Silky, non-greasy" (Claim 4, verbatim) — sensory, immediate, never a growth claim |

**Frequency framing.** The Bible locks the cadence at **2–3×/week** (Claim 3) — this is stated as
a *ritual rhythm* (a weekly self-care habit, like a hair mask or a hammam day), never as a
"prescribed dosage" or "treatment protocol." Copy should read closer to "make it part of your
weekly ritual" than "use as directed twice weekly" — the second phrasing borrows pharmaceutical
register the Bible never uses.

**Illustrative ritual vocabulary (craft terminology for internal briefs — NOT locked ad copy;
actual overlay/VO lines are sourced exclusively from Bible §6.3/§6.4):** a term like **طقوس
العناية** (*ṭuqūs al-ʿināya*, "the care ritual") is useful shorthand in a creative brief or a
shot-list annotation for "this beat is ritual-framed, not treatment-framed" — it is a working
label, not a candidate headline. `20-arabic-copywriter` must never promote an illustrative
vocabulary term from this document into a shipped overlay or VO line without it first being
locked into Bible §6.3/§6.4.

## 7. Heritage trust

Heritage is Mechat's primary trust mechanic, standing in for the clinical proof this category
usually leans on — and it must be handled as carefully as a medical claim, because a fabricated
or exaggerated heritage story is just as much an invented claim as a fabricated clinical one.

**The device.** Bible §3 positions the product as *"100% natural, authentic Moroccan
'grandmothers' recipe'"* — **وصفة الجدات** (*waṣfat al-jaddāt*, "the grandmothers' recipe") — *"at
a modern concentration."* This single phrase does three jobs: it explains *why it's natural*
(an old recipe, not a synthetic formulation), *why it's trustworthy* (survived generations of
real use, the implicit proof-of-time that stands in for a clinical trial), and *why it's warm*
(a maternal/familial voice, reinforcing the "restored femininity" trigger, Bible §6). The locked
overlay renders this directly: **«سرّ جداتنا: زيت المشاط الأحمر»** (*sirru jaddātinā: zayt
al-mishāṭ al-aḥmar*, "Our grandmothers' secret: Red Mechat Oil," Bible §6.3).

**Provenance authenticity rules — what heritage storytelling may and may not add:**

| May do (elaborate the true, general provenance) | May NOT do (fabricate a false specific) |
|---|---|
| Evoke "Moroccan herbal tradition," "an old recipe," "generations of women" | Invent a founding year, a named founder, a specific village or souk, a family surname |
| Show the hero environment's Moorish/mihrab-arch heritage cues (Bible §4) — arches, brass, warm light | Claim a specific historical certification, royal endorsement, or awarded provenance the Bible does not state |
| Use "traditional," "authentic," "grandmothers' recipe" (Bible-locked register) | Use "ancient" if it implies an unverifiable age claim beyond "traditional"; avoid invented century/era markers |
| Frame the modern product as this old recipe "at a modern concentration" (Bible §3, verbatim) | Imply modern reformulation added a new active ingredient not in the four-ingredient list (§5) |

**The ethical boundary between heritage trust and clinical proof.** Heritage trust earns its
power from being *emotionally* persuasive (a recipe that survived because it worked, told with
warmth and intimacy) — it must never be *dressed up as* scientific validation. A line like "our
grandmothers' generations of use prove it works" crosses from heritage storytelling into an
unverifiable efficacy claim wearing a heritage costume. The safe construction keeps the two
separate: heritage explains *trust and authenticity*; the four Bible-locked claims (§3 above)
carry *any* statement about what the product does. Never let one sentence try to do both jobs.

**Cultural adaptation, not appropriation, for the Gulf audience.** The product's origin is
Moroccan; its primary audience is Gulf (Saudi-first, then UAE/Oman/Egypt, Bible §3/§5). This
studio presents Moroccan heritage cues respectfully and legibly to a Gulf viewer — the
Moorish/mihrab-arch hero set, the warm herbal-remedy narrative — without collapsing Moroccan and
Gulf identity into one another or implying the product is Gulf-made. The heritage story is framed
as *received* by the Gulf audience ("a secret returns," Bible §6.4 L2) — an authentic outside
tradition being trusted and adopted, which is both accurate to the product and respectful of both
cultures (Bible §5's cultural-respect rules govern the full detail; `21-cultural-expert-gulf`
owns the audit).

## 8. COD dynamics in MENA

Cash on delivery (COD) is not a footnote payment option in this category and this region — it is
**the dominant trust mechanic** that makes a stranger's ad on Meta/TikTok/Snap feel safe enough
to order from at all, especially for a first-time buyer in a hair-loss category already burned by
"products that did nothing" (Bible §3). Every market this studio ships to defaults to COD; the
creative must treat it as a headline trust element, not small print.

**Per-market COD norms (from `config/markets.yaml`, subordinate to Bible §3's KSA-locked price):**

| Market | COD dominance | Confirmation mechanic | Inspect-before-pay | Notable risk/nuance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Saudi Arabia (primary, price-locked)** | Very high — the Bible-locked default payment method | Phone confirmation call before dispatch is standard; overlay/VO must state «الدفع عند الاستلام» | Yes — **فحص المنتج قبل الدفع** (*faḥṣ al-muntaj qabl al-dafʿ*, "inspect the product before paying"), a headline trust element (Bible §3) | The **only** market with a Bible-locked price: 139 SAR / 3-pack (was 185, −25%) |
| **UAE** | High, though card/digital penetration is higher than KSA | Phone/WhatsApp confirmation common; message both COD and prepaid, leading with COD trust | Yes | Some couriers add a small COD fee — keep the "3-pack + free shipping" value message clean of fee-related confusion |
| **Oman** | High — the dominant, trusted method in a smaller market | Phone confirmation before dispatch is standard; smaller order volumes mean higher-touch service | Yes | Delivery windows run longer outside Muscat (interior/Dhofar) — set delivery-time expectations accordingly, never over-promise speed |
| **Egypt** | Very high — COD is essential in a cash-dominant economy with low card penetration | Confirmation call is **critical**; refusal/return rates run higher than the other three markets, so intent and address must be confirmed before dispatch | Yes — a strong trust lever specifically because ecommerce trust is lower here | Higher COD refusal risk means the offer must be stated with total honesty and zero over-promising (`config/markets.yaml` risk_note) — this is the market where claims discipline (§3) and COD honesty are most tightly linked |

**Why COD belongs in the creative, not just the checkout flow.** The buyer profile from §2 is
already skeptical (burned by past products) and now being asked to trust an unfamiliar seller
seen only in a video ad. COD directly answers the risk she is actually weighing — *"what if it
doesn't work / doesn't arrive / isn't what's shown?"* — by removing financial risk entirely until
the product is in her hands. This is why the Bible states it plainly in the CTA rather than
burying it: **«اطلبي الآن — الدفع عند الاستلام»** (*uṭlubī al-ān — ad-dafʿ ʿind al-istilām*, "Order
now — Cash on delivery," Bible §6.3/CTA) and the trust-element list leads with COD, inspect-
before-pay, and the "don't like it → don't pay" refusal norm (Bible §3).

**COD honesty rules for creative:**

1. **State COD plainly, on-screen and in VO, every time — never in fine print or omitted.** It is
   a trust *feature*, and hiding it forfeits the trust benefit.
2. **Never dramatize urgency around COD** ("pay only if you love it, but hurry!") — combining a
   legitimate risk-reversal mechanic with a manufactured urgency device violates the no-fake-
   scarcity rule (§2 above, Bible §5) and muddies an otherwise honest offer.
3. **Never overstate delivery speed** beyond what `markets.yaml`'s per-market delivery_expectation
   supports (Bible §3's KSA figure: 24–48h / 1–4 business days after phone confirmation) — Egypt
   and Oman's interior/remote windows run longer, and the creative must not imply a uniform
   pan-Gulf speed.
4. **Never let the offer outrun what the confirmation call can honestly support**, especially in
   Egypt (higher refusal risk, `markets.yaml`) — an over-hyped ad that inflates expectations past
   what the confirmation call and delivered product can match directly drives refusals at the
   door, which is a real cost, not just a compliance risk.
5. **Treat "inspect before pay" as a selling point, not a hedge.** Framing it warmly ("see it,
   love it, then pay") converts a logistics detail into a confidence signal; framing it
   apologetically undercuts the very trust it's meant to build.

For the downstream measurement implications of COD (why a standard "Purchase" pixel event is
unreliable and what to optimize against instead), see `knowledge/meta-ads-playbook.md` §3's COD
measurement note — that document owns media-buying mechanics; this document owns the creative and
psychological handling of COD as a trust device.

## 10. Quick-reference checklist (for any skill drafting copy, scene direction, or a claim)

Before a headline, overlay, VO line, or beat description ships to Stage 10 (Consistency &
Negatives), confirm:

- [ ] Every claim in the line traces to one of Bible §3's four locked claims, stated no more
      strongly than the original.
- [ ] No word from the banned list (§3 table) appears — no "cure," "clinically proven,"
      "guaranteed," "regrows all," "DHT," "dermatologist," or named medical condition.
- [ ] Any stated or implied timeline matches the FAQ exactly (first use / 3–5 weeks / 2–3 months)
      and is paired with the 2–3×/week condition when it's a density/growth claim.
- [ ] Any before/after implication is carried by the single continuous camera thread — never a
      cut, split-screen, or composited image.
- [ ] No wig, extension, digital density-sweetening, or scalp-coverage retouching is described or
      implied.
- [ ] Only the four Bible ingredients are named, each with its Bible-stated function and no
      isolated "active compound" language.
- [ ] Ritual language (weekly, self-care, ritual) is used in place of treatment/dosage language.
- [ ] Heritage language stays general and evocative (traditional, grandmothers' recipe) — no
      invented founding date, founder, village, or certification.
- [ ] No heritage sentence is also doing efficacy-claim work — the two stay in separate lines.
- [ ] COD is stated plainly on-screen and in VO, never hidden, never paired with a manufactured
      urgency device.
- [ ] Delivery-speed language matches the specific market's real window (`config/markets.yaml`),
      not a generalized "fast everywhere" claim.
- [ ] Every Arabic line used is quoted verbatim from Bible §3/§6.3/§6.4, or is clearly labeled as
      illustrative internal vocabulary (§6 above) and not promoted to shipped copy without first
      being locked into the Bible.
